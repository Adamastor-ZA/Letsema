## 2. Executive summary

**Current state.** The summary runs to 2,636 words of prose and 4,574 with its tables, over nine pages (pages 10 to 18). It holds eight sections, two figures and five tables. Sixteen of its sentences reappear in the body, mostly in 2.1, 3.4, 4.1 and 4.4. It carries eleven counted lists: six constraints, six barriers, eight requirements, three paths, three shifts, three goals, six themes, twenty objectives, four questions, three indicator levels and sixteen risks.

**Three faults against section 7.1.**
1. It has no central question and no conclusion (query Q12).
2. It cannot say what the plan costs, because nothing is costed (query Q1).
3. The same point is made two or three times inside it: conversion first, seats at the peak and the 1.8 million seat figure each appear in the opening, in "What constrains growth" and again in "A viable target".

**Proposed outline**, in the order section 7.1 sets, with a word budget of about 1,450:

| Part | Content, drawn only from the present summary | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Overview | Recovery complete; 1.57 million arrivals; target 2.5 million by 2035; conversion first, seats at the peak | 110 |
| Situation | The yield premium; concentration in five markets, six months and one metro | 150 |
| Complication, opening "However," | Interest far exceeds visits; the six barriers; full routes and the runway; reach beyond the Metro; levers held nationally | 260 |
| Central question | One sentence (Q12) | 35 |
| Outlook and scenarios | Three cases with one rounding each; what separates them; Figure ES.1 | 190 |
| Recommendations | Three goals, one paragraph each; Figure ES.2 as the numbered list of the twenty canonical titles | 280 |
| Phasing, impact and funding | Three phases with dates; Gates 1 and 2; arrivals as the headline impact (Q9); funding routes and the uncosted position (Q1, Q2); the two risks rated high on both measures | 330 |
| Conclusion | Three to five sentences, ending on a short line | 90 |

**What leaves the summary** (decision 3):
- Tables ES.1 to ES.3 (about 1,400 words) restate each objective's finding. Section 7.2 allows the list and the master figure only. Proposal: move each table to the head of its goal section (3.1, 3.2, 3.3), where no overview table exists. The cost is renumbering: the three become Tables 3.1, 3.x and 3.y, and the 21 Chapter 3 tables shift.
- Table ES.4 ("What happens when") narrates the actions that Table 4.3 already lists. Proposal: delete it from the summary and keep one sentence per phase. Any detail in it that Table 4.3 lacks moves to 4.5 as prose.
- Table ES.5 (impact indicators) stays. It is the only place the summary can show the impact once, with baselines.
- "Risks to delivery" (411 words) shrinks to the two risks rated high on both measures and a pointer to 4.8.
- The paragraph on domestic travel and the Santiago and Chile evidence move to their body sections, where both already sit.

The summary is rebuilt last, in Phase 4, from the body as edited.

## 4. Organising axis and chapter order

**Axis.** The report is organised by theme: three goals, six themes and twenty objectives. Phase is the second axis and appears as columns in Table 4.1, Figure 4.1 and the action register in A7. That matches section 7.2 and stays.

**Third classification.** Initiatives (I1 to I18) are a delivery level under the objectives, not a rival classification, and Table 4.1 maps them. The risk is in the summary's eleven counted lists. The rebuilt summary keeps four: six barriers, three goals with twenty objectives, three phases and three cases.

**Chapter order.** The report follows the funnel with three departures:
1. Benchmarks (2.2) come before the diagnosis (2.3 to 2.5), not after it.
2. Scenarios sit inside 2.3 and are restated in 3.4, with no chapter of their own.
3. There is no conclusion. Section 3.4 closes the argument, and Chapters 4 and 5 follow it.

Proposal: do not move chapters or sections (decision 4). Moving 2.2 would renumber 29 figures and 21 tables that v15.1 numbered to a specification, and guardrail 5 keeps numbering as it is. The edit instead adds the central question to 1.1 and has 3.4 answer it in its closing paragraph, both built from sentences already in the report.

**Paragraphs.** Body paragraphs average 73 words. About 60% sit in the 60 to 120 word band and a third fall under 60. Short paragraphs are mostly single-point evidence statements and stay. The edit merges a short paragraph into its neighbour only where the two make one point, and splits the paragraphs over 120 words (about 37, most of them in Chapters 2 to 4) where they make two points.

## 7. Funding

**Where the argument sits now.** Funding is discussed in eight places: the summary (twice), 2.5, objective 15 in 3.3, Table 4.1, the eighteen charters in 4.3, 4.5, 4.6 and the risk register in 4.8.

**Consolidation.** Section 4.6 becomes the one funding argument, in the order the standard sets: cost, sources, net position, assumptions. Every other mention is cut to one sentence that agrees with 4.6 and points to it. The charters keep their funding-route lines, which are data.

**Contradictions and gaps found.**
1. When the rand bands are due: "before the plan is signed off" in 4.6, "before Gate 1" in the summary and Table 4.5, and "set in section 4.6" in 4.3 (Q2).
2. The net position: the plan "runs on budgets that already exist", yet a stop list must name the Wesgro work "deprioritised to fund the initiatives", and funding is "materially below peer destinations" (Q25).
3. Route support: I6 "does not create a fund" but is "scaled" with public and private money and "sized to the seats the target requires", with no amount or source (Q26).
4. Cost: no initiative is costed, so the summary cannot state a cost (Q1).

The counts reconcile: thirteen initiatives list Wesgro's operating budget as a main source and four list private capital, with three in both groups.

## 11. Decisions for the author before the edit starts

1. **Number format.** The brief defaults to `sa` (R25,9 billion; 1 498 829). The report uses `intl` throughout, as do its 41 figures, whose text is drawn into images. Under `sa` the prose alone changes at about 945 points, the tables at more, and the figures no longer match unless all are redrawn. Recommendation: `intl`.
2. **"Case" or "path".** The introducing heading and 45 uses say "case". The summary and 3.4 say "path" (20 uses), and the summary carries a sentence explaining the difference. Recommendation: "case" everywhere, with the explaining sentence deleted and the caption of Figure ES.1 changed to match.
3. **Summary tables.** Move Tables ES.1 to ES.3 to the head of 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3 and delete Table ES.4, which renumbers the Chapter 3 tables. The alternative keeps all five tables in the summary, which can still meet the word limit for prose but runs to about seven pages, not four.
4. **Chapter order.** Keep the present order and numbering. The alternative moves 2.2 behind 2.5 and renumbers Chapter 2.
5. **Recommendation template and owners.** Keep the five-part objective structure and the "Stakeholders" column. No accountabilities table and no invented owners.
