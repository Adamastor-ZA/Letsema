# 02 AFS cost structure from public information

Phase 2 deliverable. Date: 7 October 2026. Perimeter: AFS South African entities (Absa Life, Absa Insurance Company, Absa Risk Transfer Insurance Company, Absa Insurance and Financial Advisers, Instant Life, AIMS, Absa Stockbrokers and Portfolio Management, Absa Trust). Kenya and the entities sold in 2025 are excluded and shown only as reconciling items. Currency: rand millions. Basis: IFRS 17 unless stated. Tags: Fact, Derived (formula shown), Assumption (dagger). Every figure carries a data_id that resolves to document, page and URL on the Raw data tab of `afs_cost_benchmark.xlsx` and in `source_log.csv`.

## Answer first

The public record supports a cost base for the AFS underwriters of about R3.5bn on the base case, and a further R1.7bn for the advice and investment businesses on an older but disclosed basis. Insurance SA (Absa Life, AIC, ARTIC and the insurance head office) earned insurance revenue of R9 079m in FY2025 and reported other operating expenses of R1 042m, which is 11.5 percent of revenue. Inside insurance service expenses of R7 225m sit the claims, the amortised acquisition cost and an undisclosed block of attributable expenses. That block is the single biggest gap in the public record and is bracketed here at R363m to R999m, base R726m, on peer evidence.

Three findings matter for the point of view. First, the overhead sits in the non-life book and the head office. Non-Life SA carries non-attributable expenses of 12.3 percent of revenue against 4.0 percent in Life SA, and the Insurance HO column has grown from R276m to R326m in a year while reporting a loss before tax of R288m. Second, the fee paid to the bank dominates acquisition cost. Absa Bank Limited received R774m of insurance commission in FY2025, which is 56 percent of the acquisition cash flows allocated to South Africa. Strip it out and AFS acquires at 6.7 percent of revenue, which is at peer top quartile. Third, the intermediary block is larger than the underwriters' overhead. The Non-Banking Financial Services segment, which housed AIFA, AIMS, Absa Trust and the stockbroker, spent R1 724m against non-interest income of R1 104m in FY2023 and reported a headline loss of R389m. That block has not been disclosed separately since.

The addressable accounting cost base, defined as operating expense plus acquisition cost excluding the bank fee plus shareholder investment cost, is R3 524m to R4 566m, base R4 115m. Applying the precedent range of 6 to 13 percent gives a run-rate hypothesis of R211m to R594m before claims leakage, reinsurance and capital. The total hypothesis across all levers is R276m to R806m, base R505m, at a cost to achieve of R138m to R1 451m. This is a hypothesis range built from public data and peer evidence. It is not a target.

## 1. What the public record contains and what it does not

Absa discloses Insurance SA as a sub-segment of Personal and Private Banking in the results booklets, with a full insurance-format income statement for Life, Non-Life and Insurance HO, and the Group annual financial statements carry the IFRS 17 note 8 roll-forwards at group level, which includes Kenya. No AFS entity publishes standalone statements online. The KPMG South African Insurance Industry Survey carries entity tables for Absa Life Limited and Absa Insurance Company Limited to FY2024, which gives the only entity-level non-attributable expense figures. Absa Bank Limited discloses insurance commission received but does not name the counterparty. No document gives AFS headcount, policy counts, adviser numbers, expense by nature, the attributable expense split, group recharges, or FY2025 entity figures.

Three different Insurance SA perimeters appear across the booklets. The FY2023 and FY2024 booklets show Life plus Non-Life only. The 1H2025 booklet adds Insurance HO and associates. The FY2025 and 1H2026 booklets show Life, Non-Life and HO with associates removed, and the 1H2026 booklet restates FY2025 again (headline earnings R996m from R1 010m, other operating expenses R1 060m from R1 042m). The series below uses the FY2025 booklet basis from FY2024 onward and flags FY2023 as the older basis. The two cannot be spliced without an adjustment, and the adjustment is the HO column.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Revenue and cost base by line

Insurance SA revenue grew from R8 849m to R9 079m between FY2024 and FY2025, with Life at R5 310m and Non-Life at R4 102m and the HO column carrying negative revenue of R333m from intra-AFS eliminations. Insurance service expenses were R7 225m, 79.6 percent of revenue, and the insurance service result R1 655m. Other operating expenses, which are the non-attributable costs outside the service result, were R1 042m. Headline earnings fell from R1 082m to R1 010m. The reported cost-to-income ratio of 40.0 percent divides segment operating expenses by net insurance income after claims and including investment income, so it is a bank metric, not an insurer expense ratio, and should not be compared with any peer's expense ratio.

By division, Life SA is lean on overhead and heavy on claims: non-attributable expenses are 4.0 percent of revenue and the service expense ratio is 78.7 percent. Non-Life SA is the reverse: service expenses are 77.6 percent of revenue, which is a good underwriting result for personal lines, but non-attributable expenses are 12.3 percent. The HO column holds R326m of non-attributable cost, up 18 percent on the year, and this is the natural home of duplicated licence, governance and control-function cost.

The advice and investment businesses appear only in older booklets. The FY2023 Non-Banking Financial Services segment reported non-interest income of R1 104m against operating expenses of R1 724m and a headline loss of R389m. In FY2024 the block was merged with the Product Solutions Cluster head office (income R1 274m, expenses R2 073m, loss R515m), and from FY2025 it is folded into Transactions and Deposits. The intermediary cost base is therefore an FY2023 fact carried forward as an assumption, bracketed R1 500m to R1 900m.

### 2.2 Expense split

Absa does not split other directly attributable expenses from incurred claims in note 8. The disclosed components at group level are incurred claims and other attributable expenses, amortisation of insurance acquisition cash flows, and losses on onerous contracts. Allocating the group components to South Africa by each division's share of group insurance service expenses (life 88.5 percent, non-life 75.1 percent in FY2025, tagged as an assumption because Kenya's mix differs) gives allocated incurred claims and attributable expenses of R5 900m, amortised acquisition cost of R982m, and onerous losses of R356m for Insurance SA in FY2025. The attributable expense carve-out is set at 4 to 11 percent of revenue from peer disclosures (Capitec 2.6 percent, Standard Bank 9.5 percent, Liberty 11.2 percent, Sanlam 13.6 percent, Old Mutual 15.7 percent), with the base at 8 percent, which is R726m.

Expense by nature is not disclosed for AFS. The Group's own split (staff 58 percent of operating expenses) cannot be assumed to hold for an insurer. The First Assurance Kenya statements show what an entity-level split looks like (employee benefits KES 559m, IT and other contracted services inside a KES 348m other line) but Kenya is out of perimeter.

### 2.3 Operational counts

None are public. Group headcount was 37 033 at FY2025, with 26 565 in South Africa, but no insurance split exists. The FSCA register confirms AIFA, Instant Life and AIMS as authorised FSPs but does not populate representative or key individual counts. Policy counts, adviser numbers, claims counts and assets under administration for AIMS are all management information.

### 2.4 Intra-group flows

Absa Bank Limited recognised insurance commission received of R774m in FY2025, R774m in FY2024 and R739m in FY2023, all allocated to PPB. The counterparty is not named, so treating it as the fee AFS pays the bank channel is an assumption. On that assumption it is 56 percent of the acquisition cash flows allocated to South Africa in FY2025. The acquisition ratio on a cash basis is 15.3 percent of revenue including the bank fee and 6.7 percent excluding it. The Bank's related party note gives fee and commission income from fellow subsidiaries of R812m and operating expenditure with fellow subsidiaries of R1 573m, but neither names Absa Life, AIC or AIFA, so group recharges into AFS cannot be isolated and the opex ratio without recharges cannot be computed.

### 2.5 Top-down and bottom-up reconciliation

Top-down, Group insurance revenue of R11 678m less the Africa Regions segment's R2 130m gives R9 548m for South Africa, which is R469m more than Insurance SA. The difference is insurance revenue booked in South Africa outside AFS: products inside PPB that are not Insurance SA (vehicle warranties and similar), R34m in Business Banking and R54m in CIB. Bottom-up, the KPMG entity tables for FY2024 give Absa Life Limited revenue of R4 944m and AIC R3 987m, R8 931m together, which is R82m more than Insurance SA's R8 849m. The gap is the negative HO revenue, ARTIC (not a survey participant) and the Instant Life cell. On cost, entity non-attributable expenses of R765m (Absa Life R293m, AIC R472m) are R225m below the segment's R990m, which is the HO cost the entities do not carry. The three views reconcile to within the disclosed HO column, which is itself the finding: the head office is where the undisclosed cost sits.

### 2.6 Cost of capital

Segment equity of R5 540m at a cost of equity of 15.1 percent (Absa Group Integrated Report 2025) is an economic charge of R837m. Solo covers at December 2025 are 1.51 for Absa Life, 1.81 for AIC, 2.11 for ARTIC and 1.49 for the AFS insurance group. On a target cover assumption of 1.25 to 1.50 and IFRS equity as the own-funds proxy, capital above target costs R0m to R129m a year, base R79m. This is reported separately from the accounting cost base.

## 4. Addressable cost base mapped to the Phase 1 tree

Section G of the table above places FY2025 into the five pools. Pool 1 (claims and benefits, net) is R5 456m to R6 092m depending on the attributable carve-out. Pool 2 (acquisition) is R982m on the expense view and R1 387m on the cash view, of which R774m is the bank fee and R613m is other commission and adviser remuneration. Pool 3 (operating expense) is R2 905m to R3 941m, base R3 492m: R1 042m non-attributable, R363m to R999m attributable, and R1 500m to R1 900m for the intermediary block. Pool 4 is immaterial at R7m to R13m. Pool 5 is R837m of economic cost of capital, of which R0m to R129m is trapped above target cover, plus R10m to R40m for one avoidable licence.

The addressable accounting base is pool 3 plus pool 2 excluding the bank fee plus pool 4: R3 524m low, R4 115m base, R4 566m high. Section H applies the precedent savings range of 6 to 13 percent, the claims leakage band of 1 to 3 percent of net claims, a 5 to 15 percent saving on net reinsurance cost, and the trapped capital and licence lines. The total hypothesis on accounting lines is R276m to R806m, base R505m, which is roughly a third of Insurance SA profit before tax of R1 448m in the base case. Cost to achieve at precedent multiples is R138m to R1 451m. Every input is on the Normalisation tab with its rationale and can be changed.

## 5. The ten data points only management information can close

1. Expense by nature (staff, IT, premises, professional fees, group recharges) by legal entity and for Insurance HO, FY2023 to FY2025.
2. The split of insurance service expenses into incurred claims and other directly attributable expenses, by entity and by function (claims handling, policy administration, underwriting).
3. Headcount by function and entity, including the head office, and the managers-to-staff ratio by layer.
4. In-force policy counts by product and the number of product versions and rate tables in force.
5. Adviser headcount, annual premium equivalent per adviser and the adviser remuneration model for AIFA and Instant Life.
6. Commission and fees paid to Absa Bank by product, and the terms of the distribution agreement, so that the R774m can be confirmed as the AFS fee and allocated.
7. Group shared service recharges into AFS by service, with the VAT treatment of each.
8. The reinsurance programme by entity: structure, counterparties, ceded premium and recoveries, and whether placement is entity by entity or group-wide.
9. Board target solvency covers, SAM own funds and SCR by entity, so that trapped capital is measured rather than proxied.
10. The platform and vendor inventory with contract values, and the cost of each licence (board, head of actuarial function, audit, levies, returns), including the FY2025 income statement of the advice and investment businesses, which is no longer disclosed.
