## Treatments that cross chapters (use exactly these)

- **Wesgro's FY26 to FY30 targets (plan p20):** 800,000 to 1 million new international tourist arrivals; 161 business event bids worth an estimated R4.5 billion; 250 ship calls; 10 new air routes; three international tourism marketing campaigns; 12,500 international tourism trade engagements. All run over the five-year plan period. A new route is first-time scheduled international service (plan p59).
- **Arrivals metric period:** five years, FY26 to FY30, not ten. Against the base case's gain of about 304,000 arrivals between 2025 and 2030 (computed from 1,498,829 and 1,802,929), 800,000 to 1 million is 2.6 to 3.3 times the base-case gain and about twice the accelerated case's (computed). No modelled path delivers it by 2030. This sharpens the report's case that growth is a supply programme. The base year of "new" arrivals stays an open item.
- **250 cruise calls:** a five-year total in the plan, about 50 calls a year, set from a 47-call average (67 calls in 2023/24 less 20 Red Sea reroutes) rounded to 50 (plan p20). Remove the reading of 250 as one season and the argument that it needs a second berth. Close the open item on the source and horizon of the 250; keep an open item only on whether the October deck's 250 is the same figure.
- **Growth for Jobs 3 million:** Wesgro states it two ways: 3 million by 2035 (presentation to the parliamentary committee, 1 Apr 2026) and 3 million by 2030, as 2 million in 2019 plus 1 million (plan p20). Present both as Wesgro statements. Keep the existing open item on reading G4J itself.
- **Cruise value:** R1.79bn is cruise's contribution to provincial GDP-R after displacement, not "economic impact"; direct spend before displacement was about R1.99bn (StratEcon). Jobs: 1,234 direct and 2,345 in total (StratEcon pp24, 27); this closes the cruise-jobs check. International passengers rose from 55,495 to 100,255 over three seasons while total passengers fell about 12% (StratEcon pp9 to 10): value comes from mix, not volume. In 2023/24, 20 of 67 calls were Red Sea reroutes (plan p20).
- **Cape Point:** never "underused" again. It drew 940,399 visitors in 2023, up 49% (MERO Cape Metro p69), and 55.4% of international tourists visit it (PERO p68). The under-used alternatives are the receiving reserves and the Southern Line towns (Muizenberg, Kalk Bay, Simon's Town); the named alternative to Boulders is Stony Point, whose festive-season day visitors rose 28%, and Robberg 50% (PERO).
- **Flagship counts, 2023 (MERO Cape Metro p69):** Table Mountain National Park 2,795,838; Table Mountain Aerial Cableway 984,641; Cape of Good Hope 940,399; Boulders 762,494; Kirstenbosch 673,290; Robben Island 239,641. These close the flagship-count gap; carrying capacity stays open.
- **Concentration by visitors:** nine of the ten places international tourists visit most are in the Metro (PERO p68).
- **District dependence:** tourist spend equals 15.6% to 16.8% of GDPR in four outer districts against 6.7% in the Metro (MERO 2024/25). Do not use the Garden Route and Central Karoo spend-to-GDPR series (identical, a copy error).
- **Market capture (TSMA, 2022 to 2024, three-year totals; label them):** the province took about 0.1% of global tourist arrivals and 0.05% of global spend; share of air outbound trips: UK 0.27%, USA 0.19%, Germany 0.18%, France 0.18%, China with Hong Kong 0.03% (TSMA p4). The province's 3.6 million arrivals are a three-year total, about 1.2 million a year.
- **Existing programmes to build on:** the City's Tourist Safety Programme (secure zones, Tourist Police, victim support, safety forums; TDF p5); Wesgro's Advocacy and Advisory unit (plan p27); DEDAT's BDO visa study with its ranked reforms (published processing times first, then exemptions for China, India and Nigeria; Table 1.7, p34); the draft PLTF's plan to revive the MyCiTi airport service with ACSA (ch12) and MyCiTi Phase 2A to Khayelitsha and Mitchells Plain due in 2027 (p3-24); Cape Town Air Access as a partnership of the City, the province, ACSA, Cape Town Tourism, SA Tourism, Wesgro and the private sector (Letsema Update, slide 1).
- **Runway:** "one main runway" stands; the draft PLTF's "two active runways" is not used.
- **Strategy wording:** the work is research that develops an action plan; never call it a strategy. Other bodies' strategies keep their names.
