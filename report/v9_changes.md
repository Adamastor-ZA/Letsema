## 1. Chapter 3: each entry is a discussion

The "Hypothesis and disposition" part is gone from all 20 entries. Each entry keeps its kicker, title and one-sentence finding, then runs in five parts:

1. **The challenge** or **The opportunity**, whichever frames the entry better: the topic, why it matters to growth to 2035, and what holds the province back or what it can win.
2. **What the evidence shows**: the evidence table, then what it means.
3. **The course of action**: the approach and its order, then the action table (Wesgro, partners, national).
4. **Why it can work**: the mechanism, the comparator that shows it working, with its result, and the condition it depends on.
5. **Link forward**: the initiative and indicators, unchanged.

The three pillar openings now introduce each pillar as it stands: its objective, its themes and focus areas, and what it must achieve. Theme introductions no longer mention earlier wording. As in Chapter 2, no interim deliverable is named in the text; the footnotes carry them. Entries run to about 600 to 900 words of new prose.

The cold read fixed several contradictions between entries and with other chapters:

- **Focus area 12** now sends visitors to Cape Point and the under-used reserves, and manages flows at the Cableway and Boulders. v8 marketed Boulders as an alternative to itself, and its charter now matches.
- **Focus areas 6 and 7:** the capacity case on the five full regional routes now has one owner and one start: Air Access, from 2029, once the route fund runs. The I5 charter no longer carries it.
- **Focus area 4** no longer counts the hub markets' 206,584 extra arrivals as the India and China prize. It gives the two markets' own increases.
- **Focus area 7** now plans on runway relief in about 2033 throughout.
- **Focus area 10** delivers first on the Garden Route, the Winelands and the Overberg, as charter I9 does.

Six one-sentence findings changed, so the executive summary's tables changed with them, word for word:

## 3. Section 2.1: how the benchmarks were chosen

The section grew from about 2,300 to about 3,900 words of new prose. Its new subsections:

- **Benchmarks test the strategy against proven growth:** why benchmark at all, and the yardstick of the world's own growth.
- **Six comparators from a screen of 30:** the 30 destinations, the twelve criteria in four groups, the ranking, and why each of the six earned its place. A new table, "The six comparators at a glance", gives what each shares with the Western Cape, the challenge it addressed, its lead lever and its reported outcome.
- **Community cases and peer airports answer narrower questions:** how the five community cases and the eight peer airports were chosen.
- **Fast growth in 2024 was partly recovery:** why the 2024 growth rates overstate structural growth, with Santiago's decade as the test.
- **What each comparator faced and what it did:** each comparator's demand profile, the constraint it faced, its response and the result.
- **The lessons**, deepened with that evidence, each ending on what it asks of the Western Cape.
- **What the comparison cannot show:** what does not transfer, and the gaps in the evidence.

The history of how the comparator set changed is gone, from 2.1 and from Appendix A3. A3 now says how the six were chosen, under the subsection "How the six were chosen", and its table is now "How the six comparators were chosen".

## How it was done and checked

Agents drafted in six streams: Chapter 3 in four groups, section 2.1, and a sweep of every other module. Each stream was adversarially reviewed for fidelity and voice and then fixed. The rendered PDF then had a cold read by three readers, whose findings were fixed in turn.

- **Drafts:** `redteam/check_v9_draft.py` passes all 24 drafts with zero errors. It applies the house style and sentences under 36 words. It rejects any deliverable named in the text, any short-form citation and any reference to an earlier tree or a disposition. It checks that every figure and open item survives unless retired as versioning, and that no number is invented: every figure must appear in the v8 report or the evidence extracts. It also checks each Chapter 3 entry has its five parts in order.
- **Rendered text:** no reference to an earlier tree in the body or footnotes. No deliverable named in the body of the summary, Chapter 2 or Chapter 3.
- **Nothing else changed:** `redteam/diff_v8_v9.py` finds 17 modules identical to v8. The other 17 changed only in blocks named by a logged sweep edit or renumbering.
- **Build:** 146 of 146 contents entries mapped, and 845 footnotes, each defined and referenced once. All 72 table titles and 21 figure titles are three to six words, and every table row renders. `qa_structure.py` passes with zero issues; it now expects the new Chapter 3 parts and finds all 20 findings word for word in the summary. DRAFT FOR DISCUSSION is on all 210 pages.
- **Repeatable:** `redteam/apply_v9.py` rebuilds v9 from the v8 content and the drafts in `redteam/v9/drafts`.
- **Code:** the tree chart sizes its boxes without disposition labels. `qa_structure.py` and `qa_text.js` expect the new entry parts.
