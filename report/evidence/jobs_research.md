## 3. Candidate method for the Tier 1 jobs indicator

Three options, in order of preference.

1. **Provincial direct employment, regionalised TSA.** Baseline: the Western Cape's share of Stats SA direct tourism employment, following the City's method (share of tourism gross value added). Needs the provincial TSA table (Stats SA Report 04-05-07, provincial 2020 to 2022) or the PERO figure; neither retrievable here. Target: baseline grown at the rate of the Demand-Side spend projection, holding the jobs-to-spend ratio constant, on base and accelerated paths. Label: ratio-based projection.
2. **Jobs supported by international air arrivals, Wesgro ratio.** Baseline: 2 jobs per 100 international passengers applied to the 2025 base of 1,498,829 arrivals gives about 30,000 jobs supported. Target: +13,500 (base, +676,949 arrivals) and +20,300 (accelerated, +1,017,453 arrivals) by 2035. Narrow scope, but every input is Wesgro's own and the arithmetic is transparent. Does not reach the 300,000 ambition because it measures incremental jobs from international air arrivals only.
3. **Cape Town direct employment as proxy.** Baseline 106,707 (2024). Covers the Metro only, which holds 72.5% of GDPR; the report would have to state the province is larger.

Proposed treatment: carry option 2 as the Tier 1 indicator because it is Wesgro's published ratio and can be tracked quarterly from ACSA data, show option 1 as the provincial total once the PERO or provincial TSA table is obtained (open item, owner Wesgro Research), and restate the 300,000 ambition as a total-sector figure that the labour analysis under focus area 20 must test.
