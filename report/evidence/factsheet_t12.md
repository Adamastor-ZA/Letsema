### (d) Benchmark precedent

The Benchmarking Report has no case of converting hub-routed traffic from a specific unserved market, but two cases bear directly on the lever.

- Global Benchmarking Report, Mar 2026, p40 (Singapore, lever 1 "Integrated Aviation Hub Strategy and Stopover Demand Conversion"). Singapore converts transit into visits through the Singapore Stopover Programme and the Free Singapore Tour, with the Singapore Tourism Board, Changi Airport Group and Singapore Airlines collaborating "under a coordinated strategy"; outcomes listed are "Convert transit passengers into destination visitors", "Support year-round demand" and "Increase tourism receipts", with OTA platform integration (agoda, Klook, Trip.com, Expedia, Skyscanner logos). The required change for the Western Cape is to "align government, aviation, events, and private sector stakeholders under a coordinated strategy with shared KPI's and execution" (p42). Context: 13 mn international visitor arrivals in 2024 (19% increase from 2023), Mainland China the top market by spend at $4.58 billion (p39).
- Global Benchmarking Report, Mar 2026, p54, p56 (New Zealand, lever 1 "Demand-engineered air access"). New Zealand "shifted from awareness-driven marketing to route accountable demand generation", tying marketing budgets to specific long-haul corridors and designing campaigns to support route breakeven thresholds; outcomes "16,000+ incremental bookings", "(~NZD 55m ROI, 2023/24)", new route launches and frequency growth; enablers include "Government backed early-stage route incentives" and "Shared data platforms between tourism bodies and airlines". Required change: "Ring-fence budgets around priority long-haul corridors", "Formalise an Aviation Partnership Framework", "Introduce structured early-stage route support mechanisms". New Zealand's China market is "Yield Dense but Recovery Constrained" at 262 ('000s), 7,5% share and 64,3% recovery to 2019 (p53).
- Secondary: Chile's gateway-led air concentration, "Hub concentration improves long-haul route viability from distant markets" and "Tourism demand follows air access" (p25); Melbourne's demand profile names "China, India and USA" as "the greatest opportunities for future growth" and India as "one of the fastest growing markets linked to education and business travel" (p17); Kenya's Asia (India & China) share of arrivals is 12% (p32).

### (f) What the deliverables recommend

What Wesgro can do (the deliverables address these lines to Wesgro):

- Air-access actions: "secure additional frequency growth from Emirates, Qatar and Ethiopian Airlines"; "develop long-term route cases for India and China corridors"; "expand Cape Town Air Access partnership activity with coordinated airline incentives and market support" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p54; Market Assessment Report, May 2026, p58). Three levers close the deck: "India and China remain high-potential but underconnected"; "Hub carriers currently act as the province's primary global gateways" (p53).
- Relationship-building for the direct route: "develop and maintain a relationship between WESGRO and both Indian carriers and the respective Government authorities in India"; "Early relationship-building with Indian carriers and government authorities is strongly recommended" (MIDAS Aviation Market Assessment, Mar 2026, "The Indian Market Opportunity"; "Executive Summary"). For China: focus on China Southern or China Eastern, "build confidence in the seasonality of the market on a year-round basis", "demonstrate how ground products can be adjusted to meet local market requirements", and treat the market as a "long-term investment where increasing awareness leading to more indirect travel volumes needs to be implemented before any non-stop service could be considered" ("The Chinese Market Opportunity"). Begin "to engage with the airline management team in Riyadh Air over the coming years" ("The Saudi Arabian Market Opportunity").
- Market activation: "activate inside Xiaohongshu, Douyin and MakeMyTrip"; "deepen OTA partnerships with Trip.com, Almosafer and Booking.com"; "Shift investment toward trade-led conversion rather than broad awareness campaigns"; market-specific itineraries and packaged experiences (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p55; Market Assessment Report, May 2026, p59). "Test cooler-season packages with India and Gulf incentive planners" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p40).
- Coordinated action rather than campaign spend: "In practice, unlocking China, India and the Middle East requires coordinated action across access, trade and marketing, not incremental campaign spend"; two enablers, "Lower-friction access (routing, seat capacity, and smoother end-to-end travel)" and "Stronger trade channels (tour operator partnerships, packaged product, and always-on distribution)" (Current State Diagnostic, Dec 2025, p30).
- Segment and channel focus: "China and India: Strategic long-term plays requiring structural investment in air access, digital distribution, and safety perceptions"; China "WeChat and Ctrip are primary platforms", partnerships with KOLs "essential", marketing to "emphasise exclusivity, service, safety, and convenience"; India "film-inspired marketing is a viable differentiator" (Skift Segmentation Analysis, Mar 2026, Strategic Implications 1 and Selected Market Profiles [p24, p22]); Creative Connectors as the brand amplifier "in China, India, South Korea" (Key Strategic Insight [p10]). The Market Assessment's own takeout: "Structural constraints, not demand, limit growth in key high-potential markets" (India and China: "limited air access and distribution complexity") (Market Assessment Report, May 2026, p4).
- Use market-level search data to prioritise and sequence emerging markets (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, Google, p29, High; summary p9).

What partners must do (hub carriers, ACSA, airports, product owners):

- Hub carriers add frequency and capacity: the Base case assumes "Emirates, Qatar, Ethiopian and Turkish Airlines continue adding frequency and capacity into Cape Town" and the Accelerated case "hub carriers adding CPT capacity" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p19, p20); MIDAS names Guangzhou, China Southern or China Eastern as the likely Chinese entrant and Riyadh Air as a future gateway.
- Destination readiness for the two markets: "multilingual support, digital payment compatibility and culturally relevant experiences" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p55); Mandarin-speaking guides or translation support, acceptance of Alipay and WeChat Pay (p45); vegetarian options and Indian-language guides (p51); Arabic-language platforms and local currency options (p51).
- Airport capacity and the second gateway: CTIA's international terminal is a 2013 building of "~3m passengers/yr", congested at peak, with the R853m upgrade done ~2032 and runway relief ~2033 (Infrastructure Report, Jul 2026, p102, p103); the Cape Winelands airport (~R8bn, 5.2m-passenger terminal, first passengers targeted 2028) is "chasing a Middle-East anchor airline" with "No firm named carrier" and is "A high-value option, not capacity to count on" (p106, p107). The Demand-Side air-access action list includes "the potential role of the Winelands Airport development in increasing long-term regional aviation capacity" (p54).
- Attractions and venues extend their source-market reach: Robben Island Museum "would benefit from systematic inclusion in provincial and national campaigns targeting emerging source markets" (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p33, Medium); CTICC sees Asia-Pacific as underpenetrated for conventions (p45, Medium).

What sits nationally:

- Visa processing: China "Complex visa requirements"; Middle East "Slow visa processing and vague documentation" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p51); "New reforms (for example Trusted Tour Operator Scheme, e-visa expansion) are promising, but impact on Western Cape arrivals is not yet proven" (Current State Diagnostic, Dec 2025, p34); "Visa rules and border compliance" and "Air service agreements and route rights" are national-tier functions (p58); "Visa issues were cited as a constraint on growth from India and on recovery from China" (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p36, High).
- Route designation: Chinese designations are "government-to-government" and "part of international trade agreements" (MIDAS Aviation Market Assessment, Mar 2026, "The Chinese Market Opportunity"); "Restrictive bilateral air agreements are limiting airlift growth" (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p24, High).
- Border throughput: "improve immigration processing and peak-hour airport throughput at Cape Town International" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p54); "Slot congestion, limited immigration counters and slow infrastructure expansion risk capping future growth" (Inception Report, Nov 2025, p8, project-team synthesis).
- National marketing: "SA Tourism's institutional weakness is constraining implementation of otherwise sound international marketing strategies" (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p25, High).

### (f) What the deliverables recommend

What Wesgro can do:

- Work the full routes for frequency and gauge: "tactical opportunities exist for upgrades in frequency development in some markets and potentially aircraft up gauging could be encouraged from some airlines"; the doubling objective "in a very seasonal market will require the development of a clear and very clever strategy that looks for long-term gains rather than short-term wins" (MIDAS Aviation Market Assessment, Mar 2026, "In Conclusion").

### (c) Figures in the one-line reason without support

"Nigeria R31,000 and Ghana R27,700 a visit" is printed as "average spend per visitor" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p29, p30). Three caveats belong in the Chapter 3 text rather than as placeholders:

- The printed averages do not reproduce from the printed totals and visitor counts on the same slide (R216.9m / 7,256 = R29,893; R292.9m / 10,951 = R26,747; discrepancies.md B36, low); the report quotes the printed averages and footnotes the arithmetic. ### (d) Benchmark precedent

- Global Benchmarking Report, Mar 2026, p32, p34, p35 (Kenya). Kenya's arrivals mix is Africa (Uganda & Tanzania) 41%, Europe (UK & Italy) 28%, Americas (US & Canada) 16%, Asia (India & China) 12% (2024), and the deck states that "Regional African markets drive volume and business travel, while long-haul markets contribute disproportionately to leisure demand and have a higher spend per visitor"; arrivals rose from 2.089 (2023) to 2.394 (2024, thousands) with receipts of ~KSh 452.2 billion across 2.1 million arrivals (two 2024 values, discrepancies.md B41). Lever 2 positions Nairobi as "East Africa's aviation, diplomatic, and business hub" through Kenya Airways' regional hub strategy, with the transferable changes "Treat MICE as a system lever" and "Align city, provincial and national actors around gateway strategy" (p35) and the lever-2 structural outcome "Support multi-region tourism circuits across the country" (p34); JKIA carries 68% of arrivals (p32).
- Global Benchmarking Report, Mar 2026, p7: Africa's regional growth of ~8% in 2024–2025 against a global ~4% is the only pan-African figure in hand and sits above the 3–5% organic band (p7, p8).
- Secondary analogues for a regional anchor market: New Zealand treats Australia as "System Anchor & Volume Stabiliser" at 1,521 ('000s) and 43% of arrivals (p53), and Chile's South America share is 55-60% of arrivals (Argentina, Brazil; Leisure/VFR; US$1.91bn spend 2025) (p24). Neither case prints a regional-market activation mechanism beyond these roles. The Diagnostic's benchmark screen rejected Kenya at rank 12 ("Safari-driven core diverges") before the Benchmarking Report adopted it (Current State Diagnostic, Dec 2025, p61, p62; discrepancies.md B13).

### (f) What the deliverables recommend

What Wesgro can do (the Skift memo is addressed to Wesgro throughout):

- Anchor and prioritise: "South Africa must anchor the strategy"; "Nigeria and Kenya are the lead growth and yield markets", with "Nigeria is the priority for luxury, status-driven and conference travel. Kenya is the priority for business mobility, bleisure and premium urban breaks"; both "require conversion-focused, premium-positioned activation"; "Botswana and Zambia are regional conversion markets" to be addressed "through cost-effective, regionally targeted channels rather than broad brand campaigns"; "The Tier 4 markets require a brand-seeding rather than conversion approach", with Ghana and Rwanda prioritised "for initial brand-activation investment" and annual monitoring (Skift African Market Analysis, 16 Mar 2026, 5. Strategic Implications points 1 to 4 [p13]). The Demand-Side deck's implication: "Expanding African demand beyond the existing Southern African base should focus on Nigeria and Kenya, which offer the largest modelled pools of leisure and business travel opportunity" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p28); "Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana emerge as priority African growth markets" (p30).
- Positioning per market: Nigeria "Premium and luxury leisure", "Weddings, milestone travel and celebration trips", "Conferences, MICE and trade-linked itineraries", "Influencer-led and social-media-first marketing campaigns"; Kenya "Premium city breaks and short urban escapes", "Conference, trade and professional travel with leisure extensions", "Digital-first and professionally networked activation"; Botswana "School-holiday and long-weekend targeted campaigns"; Zambia "Conference and meetings travel with leisure extensions" (Skift African Market Analysis, 16 Mar 2026, 4.2 to 4.5 [p4–p8]); "Trade activation and digital are complementary, not competing" (point 6 [p13]).
- Capacity case on the full regional routes: "All five markets could be considered for further capacity increases" (Luanda, Maputo, Gaborone, Harare, Mauritius) (MIDAS Aviation Market Assessment, Mar 2026, "Seasonality"); the Base case has "Existing Wesgro programmes expanded across mature, regional and hub markets" and "India, China and Regional Africa expand through existing hub connections" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p19).
- Use Africa against seasonality: "Regional markets and business travel can counter seasonality" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p17); "Africa is a key volume stabiliser and helps smooth long-haul seasonality" (Current State Diagnostic, Dec 2025, p31).
- Extend the evidence: "Wesgro should invest in extending the Skift research scope"; "Commissioning dedicated consumer survey work for Botswana, Zambia, Ghana and Rwanda in particular would materially improve the precision of targeting" (Skift African Market Analysis, 16 Mar 2026, point 7 [p13]); "Expand research to include South Africa's domestic market and key regional African markets (Botswana, Namibia, Zambia, Tanzania, Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana)" (Skift Segmentation Analysis, Mar 2026, Recommended Next Steps 3 [p26]); validate the memo's sizing "against ... official Statistics South Africa and Cape Town Tourism arrivals data" (African memo, 3. Modelled Market Opportunity [p2]).

What partners must do (airlines, CTICC and venues, hotels, cross-border operators, Cape Town Tourism):

- Airlines add frequency or gauge on the five regional routes above 80% (Airlink, Ethiopian Airlines, Kenya Airways and the regional carriers named in MIDAS F9); Ethiopian Airlines' hub role "creates natural exposure to Cape Town as a transit and onward destination" (Skift African Market Analysis, 16 Mar 2026, 4.6 [p12]).
- Convert regional event demand: CTICC's regional African event demand is visa- and budget-constrained (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p45); "Combine Cape Town with regional stays" and prioritise off-peak bids (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p40); Nigeria's "Conferences, MICE and trade-linked itineraries" and Kenya's "Conference, trade and professional travel with leisure extensions" (African memo [p5, p6]).
- Premium product and trade channels for the Nigerian and Kenyan traveller: "five-star hotels, fine dining and exclusive experiences" (Nigeria), reliance on "travel agents and tour operators for conversion" ("Nigeria and South Africa index particularly highly on trade channels") (African memo [p5, p13]).
- Cross-border operators: C-BRTA's biannual tour-operator forums and the exposure of vetted operators on booking platforms (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p28); Johannesburg-to-Cape Town onward conversion for rest-of-Africa travellers (Table Mountain Aerial Cableway, p35, High).

What sits nationally:

- Visas for African markets: "Regional African event demand is constrained by visa requirements" (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p45, High); the Ghana visa waiver of 2024 "drove 36,656 arrivals to SA" (Skift African Market Analysis, 16 Mar 2026, 4.6 [p10]); "Visa frictions continue to constrain recovery and growth from priority markets, including India, China and parts of Africa" (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p10); "Visa rules and border compliance" are national (Current State Diagnostic, Dec 2025, p58).
- Route liberalisation: "Intra-African air travel remains expensive and fragmented and limited route liberalisation restricts competition and route flexibility" (Current State Diagnostic, Dec 2025, p31); "Restrictive bilateral air agreements" (Stakeholder Insights Report, Mar 2026, p24, High).
- Border operations: taxi-operator extortion at border posts (C-BRTA, p28, High) and the Mozambique border routes operators avoid (p28, Medium).
- National data: provincial data exist for only "the 16 African markets with available provincial data" (Demand-Side Analysis, Jun 2026, p27); the Skift Tier 4 arrivals are national, "not Cape Town specifically" (African memo [p13]); the memo's own Western Cape share is modelled at 34–35% of national foreign air arrivals.
