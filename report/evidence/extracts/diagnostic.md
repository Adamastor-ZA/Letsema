## Findings

Focus-area numbers refer to the 20 focus areas of the updated (14 October 2026) hypothesis tree. "Baseline" marks items that feed Chapter 1.2.

1. The Western Cape's stated ambition is to double international arrivals to 5.7 million and generate R54.7 billion in tourism spend by 2035. Figures: 5.7 million arrivals (p5); R54.7 billion spend (p5); target year 2035 (p5). Focus areas: baseline; 16.

2. The document frames tourism as a labour-absorbing tradable sector but prints no Western Cape tourism employment figure anywhere. Verbatim (p5, "Employment Absorption"): "With manufacturing facing headwinds, tourism is an essential mechanism for absorbing the province's semi-skilled and unskilled labour force." Verbatim (p5, "Economic Anchor"): "Tourism is designated as a primary tradable sector in the Growth for Jobs Strategy, critical for decoupling regional growth from national stagnation." Verbatim (p5, "Service Export Nexus"): "The services sector drives over 70% of provincial GVA; the strategy pivots to viewing tourism as a high-value service export." Figures: over 70% of provincial GVA from services (p5). No jobs number, employment share or source is given. Focus areas: baseline; 15; 20.

3. International two-way passenger traffic at Cape Town International Airport (CTIA) has grown strongly since 2015 and since FY17/18. Figures: "80% increase in international two-way passengers since 2015" (p7); "+29%" FY17/18 to FY24/25 (p35); "nearly 30% over eight years" (p35). Focus areas: baseline; 7.

4. CTIA handled a record international volume in calendar 2024. Figures: 3.08 million two-way international passengers in 2024 (p34); 10.4 million total passengers in 2024 (p34); "the first time international traffic exceeded 3 million" (p34); "7 % yar-on-year growth" [sic] (p34). Note the fiscal-year series on p35 shows 2,90 million for FY24/25; the two bases (calendar 2024 vs FY24/25) differ. Focus areas: baseline; 7.

5. CPT international passenger arrivals by fiscal year exceeded pre-COVID levels in FY24/25. Figures (p25, chart labels as printed, comma used as decimal separator): FY15/16 780,7k; FY16/17 956,6k; FY17/18 1,11m; FY18/19 1,19m; FY19/20 1,16m; FY20/21 80,4k; FY21/22 378,5k; FY22/23 1,07m; FY23/24 1,36m; FY24/25 1,44m; FY25/26 (to date) 746,6k. Key insight text (p25): "reaching 1.44m in FY24/25 (vs 1.16m in FY19/20)". Source as printed: "CTIA / ACSA, CPT Passenger Statistics" (p25). Focus areas: baseline; 7.

6. Direct airline access to CTIA is deepest from Africa and Europe and thinnest from Asia. Figures (p35, "AIRLINES FLYING TO CTIA PER DESTINATION"): Africa 10; Europe 9; North & South America 3; Middle East 2; Asia 1 (Singapore Airlines logo shown above the Asia bar). Source as printed: "ACSA Arrival Data, CTAA Report" (p35). Verbatim (p35): "the absence of direct Asian routes remains a critical barrier to unlocking new demand." Focus areas: 4; 6; 7.

Figures: Phase 1 capacity of 4-6 million passengers per year (p36); long-term potential to scale beyond 10 million (p36), "depending on runway extensions and terminal investment" (p36). No opening date is given. Focus areas: 7; 9; 15.

8. Overseas markets supply about nine in ten of Cape Town's air tourist arrivals. Figures (p26, "Additional Context (Q1-Q3 2024)"): total overseas arrivals to Cape Town (via air) ~535k (Jan-Aug); total Africa air arrivals ~62k (Jan-Aug); overall air arrivals growth vs 2023 +6%; overseas share of arrivals ~90%. Note (p26): "Figures reflect tourist arrivals to Cape Town via air and are used as a proxy for Western Cape performance." Focus areas: baseline; 5; 6.

9. The UK, USA and Germany are Cape Town's three largest overseas air markets, and Cape Town's recovery against 2019 outpaced South Africa's in 8 of the top 10 markets (Jan-Jun 2024). Figures (p26, thousands, CPT via air / SA): UK 89 / 184; USA 77 / 188; Germany 76 / 129; Netherlands 29 / 59; France 20 / 63; Canada 12 / 29; India 12 / 42; Switzerland 11 / 20; Australia 10 / 46; Italy 9 / 20. Cape Town air arrivals extracted from Wesgro IQ Tourism Performance Dashboard." (p26). Focus areas: baseline; 5.

10. Cape Town's overseas demand is concentrated in mature long-haul markets, with the top five accounting for 61% of arrivals (Jan-Aug 2024). Figures (p29): UK 18%; USA 18%; Germany 15%; Netherlands 6%; France 4%; Italy 3%; India 3%; Canada 3%; Australia 2%; Switzerland 2%; "Other markets = 26% of arrivals"; "The top 5 source markets make up 61% of total arrivals". Source as printed: "Wesgro Research, Cape Town Tourism Visitor Trends: Jan-Aug 2024 (Stats SA)." Note (p29): "Shares and recovery are based on Cape Town overseas air arrivals (Jan-Aug), not provincial (WC) totals." Focus areas: baseline; 5.

## Definitions and method

- Nature of the document (p3): literature-based data synthesis of existing sources; not new market research; not a supply-side readiness study or infrastructure audit; not the strategy.
- Pillars (p6): "Managing and Enhancing Demand", "Diversifying Product Supply", "Strengthening the Enabling Environment"; renamed in the tree (p64) as Reshape Demand, Transform Supply, Build Enabling Capability.
- Fiscal-year convention: CTIA/ACSA passenger series are by fiscal year FY15/16 to FY25/26 "(to date)" (p25) and FY17/18 to FY24/25 (p35). No FY start month is stated. Calendar 2024 is used for the 3.08 million / 10.4 million CTIA figures (p34).
