## Findings

1. The assessment covers 50,332 tourism assets across six Western Cape district regions, classified into three system layers (attractions, services, infrastructure). Figures: 50,332 (p2, p3, p10); "~50,332" (p4); 6 regions (p2, p3, p4, p10). Focus areas: 10, 18, 19.

2. The report positions itself under the "Transform Supply" pillar and names six focus areas it addresses: Close the Air Access and Cruise Capacity Gaps; Accelerate Tourism-Enabling Internal Mobility; Lead Removal of Infrastructure, Visa and Safety Deficits; Support Diversified Route and Product Development; Align Visitor Experience In-Destination; Balance Impact on Nature-Based Attractions. Figures: six focus areas in two groups (p3). Focus areas: 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 19.

3. The executive summary states growth is supply-constrained: "Growth is supply-constrained, demand exists but infrastructure gaps prevent conversion" and "Dispersal needs deliberate corridor investment, not organic spillover from Cape Town" (p5). Figures: none. Focus areas: 9, 10, 15.

4. Three established corridors (N2, R62, Winelands) "form a structural backbone for visitor flow"; infrastructure is "eroding rapidly with distance from Cape Town" and there is "over-reliance on the N2 spine and private car access that limits regional dispersal" (p5). Figures: 3 corridors (p5). Focus areas: 9, 10.

5. The executive summary names shoulder-season events as "the single largest lever to flatten the demand curve" and says events programming is "critically thin in shoulder and off-peak seasons"; "Events and nature-heritage are the twin levers to year-round, spatially distributed growth" (p5). Figures: none. Focus areas: 2, 10, 12.

6. "A data layer is the prerequisite without it, no intervention can be targeted, measured and impactful"; "Data and digital gaps undermining the ability to plan, market, and measure performance" (p5). Figures: none. Focus areas: 3, 18.

7. The supply system is modelled as three layers (Attractions = why travel; Services = what to do; Infrastructure = how to access) and "Destination performance is constrained by the weakest layer" (p9). Figures: 3 layers (p9). Focus areas: 9, 10, 11.

8. The asset taxonomy has 12 categories under the three layers, each with named subtypes (full table below) (p10). Figures: 3 layers, 12 categories (p10). Focus areas: 10, 18.

9. Phase 1 criteria listed: Accessibility, Service quality, Service readiness, Commercial viability, Sustainability, Accessibility (listed twice) (p13). Figures: 3 dimensions (p11); 6 criteria lines (p13). Focus areas: 10, 18.

Tools shown: Overpass dashboard, QGIS interface and QGIS mapping (p14). Figures: 4 phases (p12). Focus areas: 18.

Figures: ~1.5m (p19); 20.2%; 37.5%; 11.2% (p19). Focus areas: 5, 10.

23. 24. Figures: 4 and 5-star (p32). Focus areas: 1, 2, 3, 10, 15.

25. Figures: 5 secondary nodes (p33). Focus areas: 11, 13.

32. Structural barrier: self-drive dependence. Figures: 3 named destinations (p41). Focus areas: 7, 9.

33. Structural barrier: concentration. "Tourism infrastructure and services remain concentrated in Cape Town, the Winelands and the Garden Route"; "Secondary destinations struggle to attract tourism investment due to lower visitor volumes and weaker supporting infrastructure"; "Additional visitor growth is increasingly placing pressure on roads, parking, accommodation and tourism services within already-concentrated corridors"; summary: "Growth is reinforcing existing tourism hubs rather than dispersing economic benefit" (p41). Figures: none. Focus areas: 12, 15.

34. Structural barrier: weak diversification and climate exposure. Figures: none. Focus areas: 2, 10, 14.
