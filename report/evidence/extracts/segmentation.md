## Findings

Focus-area numbers in square brackets refer to the 20 focus areas of the 14 October 2026 hypothesis tree.

1. The analysis is built on Skift Proprietary 2025 Travel Consumer Quantitative Market Research, a survey of more than 25,000 outbound travellers across 34 markets, fielded in May 2025 and applied to 17 priority markets for Wesgro. Figures: 25,000+ travellers (p2, p3, p27); 34 outbound markets (p2, p3, p27); 17 priority markets (p2, p3); fieldwork May 2025 (p3, p27); n=800 to n=1,600 per market (p3); accuracy ±3.0 percentage points at 95% confidence (p3, p27), the phrase "19 times out of 20" appearing on p3 only. [1, 4, 5]
2. The segmentation is values-based (motivations, attitudes, behavioural patterns), not demographic, and a South Africa destination-interest filter converts global segments into a destination-relevant addressable audience. Figures: travel consumer defined as adults 18+ with at least one overnight trip in the 24 months prior to fieldwork, "UN Tourism-aligned" (p3); clusters "weighted for travel frequency, long-haul propensity, and spending power" (p3). [1]
3. The research covers 17 international markets only and excludes South Africa's domestic market and seven neighbouring African markets. Figures: excluded markets Botswana, Namibia, Zambia, Tanzania, Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana (p3); recommended next step 3 is to expand research to these markets (p26). [6]
4. Four high-value segments are identified with global addressable sizes of ~30M, ~25M, ~19M and ~23M long-haul-capable travellers. Figures: Curious Explorers ~30M; Creative Connectors ~25M; Discerning Connoisseurs ~19M; Reflective Adventurers ~23M (p2, p4, p5, p8, p11, p14, p17); defined as "Estimated addressable long-haul capable travellers across 34 analysed outbound markets" (p4). [1]

### T17. Key Strategic Insight boxes (verbatim)

Curious Explorers (p7): "Curious Explorers are the largest and most culturally aligned segment for the Western Cape, representing the most scalable growth opportunity while maintaining destination authenticity and sustainability alignment. They generate consistent year-round visitation, strong regional dispersal, and longer stays — and are anchored in the Western Cape's most important source markets: the UK, Germany, USA, France, and the Netherlands. This segment should anchor cultural destination positioning and long-haul travel marketing across European and North American markets."

Creative Connectors (p10): "Creative Connectors are Cape Town's most powerful brand amplifiers. Though their per-trip spend is slightly lower than luxury segments, they travel more frequently, generate disproportionate earned media value through social sharing, and shape destination desirability for a new generation of travellers. They are also more likely to extend their trip to include luxury safari experiences, increasing total in-country spend. The Western Cape's combination of world-class wine estates, signature gastronomy, and dramatic natural beauty positions it as a credible premium competitor for this traveller — particularly in the US, UK, France, Germany, UAE, and among China's growing affluent independent travel market."

Reflective Adventurers (p16): "Reflective Adventurers are the Western Cape's most important segment for regional dispersal and shoulder-season management. They stay longer, travel further from Cape Town's urban core, and actively seek out the province's less-commercialised natural assets. Their strong sustainability values align with the region's conservation agenda, and their tendency to travel year-round — including quieter winter months — makes them essential for smoothing seasonality and supporting tourism economies in the Winelands, Garden Route, and Overberg."

## Definitions and method

- Data source: "Skift Proprietary 2025 Travel Consumer Quantitative Market Research (Skift Research)" (p2, p3, p27). Every table is sourced to "Skift Research". No other data source (Stats SA, ACSA, OAG, SAT, Mero, StratEcon, Census, OSM) is cited anywhere in the document. The only external references are "UN Tourism-aligned" for the travel-consumer definition and "ESOMAR international standards" for research conduct (p3, p27), and "census data in each market" as the basis for stratified sampling (p3).
- Fieldwork: May 2025, online research panels, stratified random sampling aligned to current census data in each market (p3). Findings "reflect respondent views at the time of fieldwork (May 2025)" (p27).
- Base year / period: survey year 2025; analysis "as at December 2025 / February 2026" (p27); document dated March 2026 (p1). No fiscal-year convention; all periods are calendar.
- Universe: 25,000+ outbound travellers, 34 outbound markets; 17 priority markets analysed for Wesgro (p2, p3). The 17 markets are: United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Netherlands, Spain, Switzerland, Belgium, Australia, China, India, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, UAE, United States, Canada (p19).
- Sample per market: n=800 to n=1,600 (p3). Accuracy ±3.0 percentage points at 95% confidence (p3, p27).
- Travel consumer: adults 18+ with at least one overnight trip in the 24 months prior to fieldwork (p3). "TTC" (Total Travel Consumer base) is used for China: ~330M (p22).
- Segmentation basis: values-based clustering on motivations, attitudes and behavioural patterns; "Advanced statistical clustering ... validated across regions and weighted for travel frequency, long-haul propensity, and spending power" (p3).
- Addressable Pool: "Total Travel Consumer base × Segment Incidence × South Africa Interest Rate" (p5). "Global addressable" is "Estimated addressable long-haul capable travellers across 34 analysed outbound markets" (p4). Total Travel Consumer bases per market are not printed except China.
- Segment Incidence: share of a market's travel consumers in the segment (column heading, p5, p8, p11, p14, p20–21). SA Interest Rate: share with interest in travelling to South Africa (p3 "Destination interest filter"; column heading p5 etc.).
- Spend: "Avg long-haul trip spend" in $ (currency not named) (p5, p8, p11, p14, p17).
- LOS: "nts" = nights; "Most common LOS" in segment profiles; market profiles use "days" (p21–22).
- Scope limits: 17 international markets only; South Africa domestic and Botswana, Namibia, Zambia, Tanzania, Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana excluded (p3); results "directional rather than definitive" (p27); "primary research fully customised to Wesgro's objectives and audiences would be required to validate and refine these insights" (p27).
- Ability-to-Win Score: out of 5.0, "Assessed across product portfolio alignment, brand perception fit, competitive saturation, and air access ease" (p18); no weighting or scoring method is printed.
- Organisation naming as given: "Wesgro"; "Skift Research"; "Skift Inc." (header); "CapeNature"; "UN Tourism"; "ESOMAR"; "British Airways Holidays"; "Virtuoso"; "Leading Hotels"; "WeChat"; "Ctrip".
