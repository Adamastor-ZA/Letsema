## Findings

1. The assessment's purpose is "To identify which township and rural communities are missing out on tourism income, to understand why and identify practical routes to stimulate development", built in six steps: which communities, asset inventory, four archetypes, one binding constraint, six pathway types, what Wesgro can move. Figures: 107 township and rural communities long-listed; 20 shortlisted "distributed 3-5 per region"; 6 profiled (p3). Focus areas: 13.

2. Inclusion is defined by location of the activity, not by who earns: counts as inclusion are tourism activity located in the community (a tour, a meal, a bed, a performance, a market), assets in the community that visitors use, events held inside the boundary, visitor spend landing with businesses trading from the community, jobs inside that activity held by residents, and businesses counted by where they trade whatever their size; lesser or no inclusion is a resident commuting to a hotel job in the node, a township supplier delivering to a node while no visitor enters ("procurement, not presence"), a drive-through tour, a festival named for the community but staged in town, and a community trust holding equity that never pays out (p4). Focus areas: 13.

3. Four archetypes are set by a 45-minute drive-time threshold and whether anything sits inside the boundary; the long-list counts differ between slides. Figures: Proximate, assets underused: within 45 minutes with at least one attraction inside, "48 on long-list" (p4) but 49 (p15, p17); Proximate, no assets: 43 (p4, p15, p17); Remote, assets underused: beyond 45 minutes or named remote by Wesgro, "8 on long-list" (p4) but 7 (p15, p17); Remote, no assets: 8 (p4, p15, p17). Shortlist by archetype: 11, 6, 2, 1 (p15). Focus areas: 13, 10.

4. The 20 shortlisted communities hold a large share of people and almost none of the traced foreign spend. Figures: 18.4% of Western Cape residents (Census 2011); 15.4% of employed residents (Census 2011); 0.02% to 0.04% of "foreign direct spend traced to businesses trading inside (floor)" (p5). Focus areas: 13.

5. Capturing 1% of foreign direct spend would redirect R259m a year. Figures: "Documented today (floor)" R4m to R10m a year; "1% of FY2025 foreign direct spend" R259m; "1% of the 2035 spend ambition" R547m (p5, bar chart values printed on bars). Focus areas: 13, 15.

6. The long-list of 107 covers almost half the province's 2011 population and most of it sits near existing tourism geography. Figures: 2.81 million people in 2011 (48% of the province), 3.79 million on the 2025 estimate; 93 of 107 within 45 minutes of a node or corridor; 35 supply-report nodes and three corridors (N2, R62, Winelands roads); remote communities cluster on the upper West Coast, behind Piketberg and off the N1 (p16). Focus areas: 13.

7. Long-list entry rule: every Census sub-place cluster with 1,500 people or more and official unemployment of 25% or more, plus named mission, fishing and farm settlements (p14). Focus areas: 13.

8. Five weighted criteria score the long-list. Figures: Need 0.25; Tourism Potential 0.20; Proximity 0.20; Readiness 0.20; Access 0.15; plus an archetype rule (p14; full thresholds in Key tables). Focus areas: 13.

9. The shortlist size is stated inconsistently. Figures: "18 Shortlisted, three per region, chosen on composite score, coverage of four archetypes specific remote cases" (p14); "20 communities shortlisted, distributed 3-5 per region" (p3); "20 shortlisted: five in Cape Metro and three in each district" (p16); table of 20 (p18); "Of the 20 communities" (p28). Focus areas: 13.

10. Regional distribution of long-list and shortlist. Figures: Cape Metro 26 long-listed, 5 shortlisted; Cape Winelands 16, 3; Overberg 19, 3; Garden Route 23, 3; West Coast 13, 3; Central Karoo 10, 3 (p16). Focus areas: 13.

11. Asset depth is weak and unevenly distributed relative to population. Figures: mapped attractions per 10,000 residents, Langa 1.0, Mitchells Plain 3.7, Stellenbosch town 51; Mitchells Plain's 100 mapped attractions are 89 natural features at Wolfgat and the coast, 7 activity, 3 cultural and 1 commercial; Atlantis held as remote at 26 minutes from Darling and 45 from city centre; Worcester is not a node in the supply report, so Zweletemba and Avian Park (60,000 people) score remote (p17). Focus areas: 10, 13.

12. Shortlist composite scores run from 1.80 to 3.00 and 2011 unemployment from 14% to 55%. Figures: Langa 3.00 and Khayelitsha 3.00 highest; Prince Albert North 1.80 lowest; unemployment De Rust 55% highest, Prince Albert North 14% lowest; 2025 population estimates from 1,568 (Elands Bay) to 639,276 (Khayelitsha) (p18; full table in Key tables). Focus areas: 13.

13. Organisation, not distance, is the most common binding constraint. Figures: 11/20 "Organisation is the binding constraint"; 9/20 "Communities record a safety-perception gap"; 5/20 "Communities record extortion"; 0/20 "Distance is the binding constraint" (p30). The appendix tables assign one binding constraint per community: Organisation 11, Attraction 6, Safety 1, Market link 1, Infrastructure 1 (count from p53-p58). Focus areas: 13, 19.

14. Half the shortlist has no identified active tourism body. Figures: headline "10 lack an identified active tourism body, 9 show no visible resident benefit, and 6 have recorded asset damage" (p28); the table on the same slide shows Gap in 10 cells for Organisation present, 9 for Benefit visible to residents, and 5 for Assets protected or damaged (p28; Developing cells carry further damage records for Atlantis, Gugulethu and Kayamandi). Focus areas: 13.

15. Safety is the most widespread readiness gap. Figures: Safety perception Gap in 9 communities and Extortion Gap in 5 (p27 table); 465 murders in 2025/26 in Khayelitsha's three precincts; 218 in Mitchells Plain's three precincts; 213 in Gugulethu; 68 precinct murders in Langa; Atlantis 50 murders in 2024 and 38 in 2025/26 (p41); Kayamandi "14 shooting deaths from May to July 2026" (p27) but "11 shooting deaths May to Jul 2026" (p21); Mbekweni precinct crime up 14.7% to 1,935; Thembalethu up 12.3% to 3,119; Genadendal 731 recorded crimes; Laingville 770 and 3 murders; Elands Bay 174 and no murders; Doringbaai no murders or robberies; Prince Albert North 693 (p27, p51); Kwa-Mandlenkosi has no police station inside and "a constable was murdered in Gaba Road in Aug 2025" (p50); Kassiesbaai's nearest SAPS is 24 km away at Bredasdorp with no incidents recorded (p27, p44). Focus areas: 19, 13.

16. The benchmarks point to one order: "a seller with a stake, a safe and workable visit, product that keeps spend in the community, then exposure" (p12). Six takeaways: a sustainable local operator is essential; resident ownership matters; safety is a prerequisite; capture local spend; sell what already exists; track spending (p12). Focus areas: 13, 19.

17. Rio de Janeiro: access and safety were preconditions, not product. Figures: Rio Top Tour launched Santa Marta 30 August 2010 for about 5,000 residents; first phase about R$230,000 (R$184,000 federal, R$46,000 state); 50-person guide workshop, bilingual map of 34 sites; about 4,500 visitors in first month; by 2015 about 20 resident guides ran Santa Marta and jeep firms had left; 81% of foreign visitors spent up to R$10 in Rocinha (2014); Cantagalo elevator about R$89 million; Providência cable car R$75 million within R$163 million package, refurbishment 2021 to 2025 R$42 million, idle eight years; 38 police units and almost 10,000 officers at peak; 544,000 visitors to Rocinha in 2023; security collapsed in 2017 and police shot a tourist; public support was withdrawn early and the funicular was often out of service; planned expansion to four additional favelas did not take place; Sebrae provided business support and Investe Rio credit; recovery since 2023 is "resident & social-media led" (p7). Focus areas: 13, 19.

18. Medellín Comuna 13: mobility infrastructure built for residents became the stage for a resident product, but governance and capacity limits came late. Figures: Metrocable Line J (2008) about 96.9 billion pesos, about 35,000 passengers per day; six outdoor escalators (2011) about 10 billion pesos, maintenance above 260 million pesos a year, replacing about 350 steps for around 12,000 residents; integrated urban programme 65 billion pesos across 22 projects by 2010, more than 100 billion by 2020; Graffitour 2011, Casa Kolacho 2013; visitors 1.66 million in 2022 and 1.98 million in 2025, 52% international; more than 1,000 businesses and about 300 professional guides; 2023 carrying-capacity study (110 million pesos) estimated 667 visitors per day (p8). Focus areas: 9, 13, 19.

19. Vilakazi Street: public-realm upgrades amplified existing assets but benefits concentrated on street-front owners. Figures: Mandela House opened 1997 and was restored 2008 to 2009; Hector Pieterson Museum opened 2002, cost R23.2 million (national government and City of Johannesburg); residents opened Nambitha (1999) and Sakhumzi (2001); JDA approved a precinct plan in 2008; public-realm works (paving, lighting, trees, memorial walls, public art) completed 2009 and 2010, cost not publicly disclosed; Mandela House restoration about R9 million (corporate donors); Orlando Stadium about R280 million; 25 CCTV cameras (2017); about 1,000 tourists per day by January 2010; Mandela House 37,712 visitors in four months to March 2020; Sakhumzi from four employees (2001) to about 110 (2019); ten restaurants more than 800 jobs; 13 guesthouses formed an association; company owning Mandela House entered liquidation (p9). Focus areas: 13.

20. Costa Rica: a management entity with its own selling channel was the engine and it did not survive donor exit. Figures: lodges on UNDP GEF small grants from the 1990s; COOPRENA (1994) and ACTUAR (2001); Law 8724 (2009) confined community rural tourism to associations and cooperatives, regulation 14 months late; ACTUAR turnover more than USD 800,000 in 2013 for more than 30 member communities; ACTUAR dissolved end of 2018, "no reason has been identified"; on Chira island ten women built the La Amistad lodge from 2001; benefits "stopped at association members" and no state funding for the selling network was identified. The deck's prescription for the Western Cape (p10): Doringbaai and Elands Bay "need the entity before more product. Give it its own selling channel and a succession plan that does not rest on a donor", and "write membership rules so wages and supply contracts reach non-members, and price access and transfers into the product" (p10). Focus areas: 13.

21. Kliptown is the warning case for every anchor asset: capital of national rank, no operating income. Figures: Province and City first allocated R436 million; JDA received about R497 million 2001 to 2010 plus R40 million to 2013; Walter Sisulu Square R160 million to R180 million; hotel R24 million (PPP, 48 rooms, IDC finance, community trust shareholder); R60 million surrounding infrastructure and R20 million refurbishment (2015); institutional sequence: Gauteng named Kliptown a priority in 2000, JDA implementation from 2001, design competition 2002, construction from June 2003, Walter Sisulu Square opened 26 June 2005, management transferred to Johannesburg Property Company 2006, 48-room hotel opened 2007; business case projected almost 200,000 international visitors a year; by 2017 about 4,000 museum visitors a year; local small businesses received 5% of construction contract value; looting 2021 (p11). Focus areas: 13, 15.

22. Each archetype carries its own response. Figures (p15): Proximate, assets underused: "Integrate: itinerary slots, procurement, events; organise sellers" (benchmark Vilakazi, Rio); Proximate, no assets: "Create one anchor asset with covenant, then integrate" (benchmark Kliptown); Remote, assets underused: "Open access & place asset on route product" (benchmark Comuna 13); Remote, no assets: "Procurement & skills first; asset only with route product" (benchmark Costa Rica). Restated on p31 as first moves. Focus areas: 13, 10.

23. Common design requirements and a six-step common sequence apply across archetypes. Figures: requirements are functioning selling mechanism, established route to market, site-specific safety plan, baseline from first test, viable model before assets; sequence 1 validate offer, 2 establish booking route, 3 complete safety plan, 4 test on fixed date, 5 invest after demand, 6 scale through channels (p31). Focus areas: 13, 3.

24. The core model has three parts and a pilot of two to three communities. Figures: 1 functioning selling capability (maintain product information; receive enquiries and bookings; coordinate reliable delivery; record bookings and local revenue); 2 connection to existing demand (tourism channels; existing itineraries; events and visitor experiences; accommodation and establishments); 3 activation and investment gates (site-specific safety and access plan; viable operating model; operating budget; maintenance and marketing covenant); "PILOT 2–3 OF 6. Profiled communities form the shortlist"; four steps select on readiness, test the model, measure results, scale after thresholds are met; scale conditions "bookings, local spend, operational delivery and safety meet thresholds agreed before launch" (p32). The six exemplars are Langa, Kayamandi, Zwelihle, De Rust, Doringbaai and Laingsburg (p14). Focus areas: 13.

25. Selling capability is defined as four elements with Langa as illustration. Figures: accurate product information; enquiries and bookings ("One live route"); delivery and quality; measurement ("Bookings, visitors and locally retained revenue"); first deliverable "One live booking route, published operating dates, a named customer contact and a simple record of bookings and local revenue" (p33). Focus areas: 3, 13.

26. Demand connection runs through existing channels under explicit commercial terms. Figures: channels are itinerary or booking channel, accommodation package, event programme, established visitor experience; commercial terms "Commission, fees, revenue share and payment arrangements"; conversion measurement "Referrals, enquiries, bookings and cancellations"; Zwelihle immediate opportunity: four resident guides sell by phone, no shared booking channel or venue (p34). Focus areas: 3, 11, 13.

27. Safety and access are launch gates. Figures: four gates arrival (confirmed entry point), defined route, visitor activity (operating date and hours), departure (confirmed exit arrangement); local measurement uses incidents inside the visitor precinct and operational logs, with "SAPS station totals as context only"; "tourism activity alone cannot resolve crime or extortion. A domestic market test validates the visitor experience and operating model. A formal safety assessment remains a separate launch requirement" (p35). Focus areas: 19, 13.

## Definitions and method

- Inclusion test (p4): counted inside the community boundary only; businesses counted by where they trade, whatever their size; jobs counted inside tourism activity held by residents.
- Community unit: Census 2011 sub-place clusters (p14); Mitchells Plain is twelve sub-places (p22), Knysna north nine sub-places (p47); Kassiesbaai is reported at Arniston main place (p44).
- Base year: Census 2011 for population, unemployment, income and housing, because "Census 2022 is not published below municipality & ward & asked no income question" (p15); the Western Cape Government PDO 2025 population estimate is shown alongside as a size update (p5, p15, p16). Some profiles cite Census 2022 municipal figures (Zwelihle 29,997 per IDP, p23; Mitchells Plain services 2022, p22; Khayelitsha 40.5% informal 2022, p40).
- Fiscal conventions: SAPS crime figures are 2025/26 station data via ISS Crime Hub (p22, p27, p28, p30, p48, p51); spend scenario uses "FY2025 foreign direct spend" and "the 2035 spend ambition" (p5); municipal budgets cited by municipal financial year (e.g. 2026/27, 2027/28).
- Proximity: free-flow drive times from OSRM, measured from the community side to the nearest supply-report node or corridor (p16, p18; "free-flow desktop estimate" p17); peak ranges given for Cape Metro (p18, p53); 45 minutes is the proximate/remote threshold; Atlantis is held remote by Wesgro naming at 26 minutes (p17, p53).
- Demand nodes and corridors: 35 nodes and three corridors (N2, R62, Winelands roads) taken from "the supply report (31 Jul 2026)" (p16); pipelines also from "supply report pipelines 31 Jul 2026" (p22, p48-p51).
- Asset inventory: OpenStreetMap Geofabrik extract of 16 Sep 2026 (p22, p48, p49, p50, p51), counted inside the boundary and within a 5 km buffer, by layer (attractions, accommodation, eating and retail, transport and enabling); rail within 1.5 km and stops within 500 m; counts "pending confirmation"; known artefact: OpenStreetMap holds no place node for Kwa-Mandlenkosi (p50).
- Readiness ratings: Ready / Developing / Gap, "ratings from the community side, LTO confirmation outstanding" (p27, p28).
- Spend floor: "Foreign direct spend traced to businesses trading inside (floor)" (p5); method of tracing not described in the deck.
- Scope limits: desk research 17 Sep 2026 (p20-p26, p40-p51) or 17 to 23 Sep 2026 (p22, p27, p28); LTO confirmation outstanding; no employment split local versus commuted published for any community; interventions are "illustrative" and most have no budget line (p36); a formal safety assessment is a separate launch requirement (p35).
- Sources the document cites by name: Statistics South Africa (Census 2011 sub-places); Western Cape Government PDO 2025 estimate; SA Tourism; Demand-Side Analysis 16 Sep 2026; Diagnostic Report v2; supply report 31 Jul 2026; OpenStreetMap (Geofabrik 16 Sep 2026); OSRM; SAPS 2025/26 via ISS Crime Hub; municipal IDPs, SDFs and LED strategies (City of Cape Town IDP 2022-27, Stellenbosch IDP 2022-27, Overstrand IDP 2025/26, Oudtshoorn IDP 2026/27, Saldanha Bay IDP 4th review May 2026, Cederberg SDF Jul 2023, Beaufort West draft IDP 2026/27, Prince Albert LED Document 5 Apr 2017, and others); MERO 2024 and 2025/26 (p45, p50); SEP-LG 2023 and 2024 (p49, p50, p51); Wesgro Cape Karoo visitor trends 2023 and Jan to Jun 2024 (p50, p51); Wesgro 2018 Paarl visitor data (p42); City Mitchells Plain Economic Area Profile Jun 2025; WCPP report 18 Feb 2020; GroundUp; local press (plainsman.co.za, novanews.co.za, cbn.co.za, engineeringnews.co.za, IOL); operator and municipal websites.
