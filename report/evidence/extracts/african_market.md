## Findings

Focus-area numbers refer to the 14 October 2026 hypothesis tree (1–20).

1. The memo identifies five priority African source markets for Cape Town and the Western Cape with a combined estimated annual addressable adult market of 4.5 million to 6.4 million trips. Figures: 4.5 million to 6.4 million trips (p1, Executive Summary). Focus areas: 6.

2. South Africa is given as the dominant share of the African opportunity, with four slightly different formulations of the share. Figures: "more than 80% of this opportunity by volume" (p1, five-market basis); "approximately 80% or more of the total African adult travel opportunity for the Western Cape by volume" (p4, 4.1); "approximately 75–80%" of the twelve-market total (p3, note under the table); "more than 75–80% of the realistic African adult travel opportunity" (p13, Section 5 point 1). Focus areas: 6.

3. The markets are tiered into four tiers in the table, although the introductory sentence says "three tiers". Figures: Tier 1 South Africa; Tier 2 Nigeria, Kenya; Tier 3 Botswana, Zambia; Tier 4 Zimbabwe, Tanzania, Ghana, Uganda, Ethiopia, Mozambique, Rwanda (p2); "structured into three tiers based on scale, growth potential, proximity and commercial fit" (p1). Focus areas: 6.

4. Nigeria's annual addressable adult market is 310k to 510k, split between leisure and business. Figures: leisure 220k – 360k; business 90k – 150k; total 310k – 510k; role "Tier 2 — Premium growth market" (p2, Section 3 table). Focus areas: 6.

5. Kenya's annual addressable adult market is 240k to 400k. Figures: leisure 170k – 280k; business 70k – 120k; total 240k – 400k; role "Tier 2 — East African business + leisure" (p2, Section 3 table). Focus areas: 6.

6. South Africa's annual addressable adult market is 3.7m to 5.1m, treated as a combined domestic and regional market rather than conventional outbound. Figures: leisure 2.8m – 3.8m; business 0.9m – 1.3m; total 3.7m – 5.1m (p2); "2.8 million to 3.8 million adults annually, with a further 0.9 million to 1.3 million in the business and meetings segment" (p4); "South Africa functions as both a domestic and regional market for the Western Cape. Its sizeable addressable market reflects the full short-haul, domestic leisure, VFR and business travel base, not conventional long-haul outbound behaviour." (p3). Focus areas: 6.

7. Botswana and Zambia are sized as Tier 3 regional markets. Figures: Botswana leisure 80k – 130k, business 25k – 45k, total 105k – 175k, "Tier 3 — High-value regional market"; Zambia leisure 70k – 120k, business 25k – 40k, total 95k – 160k, "Tier 3 — Regional support market" (p3, Section 3 table). Focus areas: 6.

8. Seven Tier 4 markets are sized, with Ghana at 55k to 100k. Figures: Zimbabwe 180k – 290k; Tanzania 65k – 115k; Ghana 55k – 100k; Uganda 30k – 60k; Ethiopia 25k – 50k; Mozambique 65k – 120k; Rwanda 25k – 50k (p3, Section 3 table; the same ranges are repeated in the "EST. W. CAPE ADDRESSABLE" column of the Tier 4 table, p9–p12). Focus areas: 6.

9. The twelve-market combined total is 4.89m to 7.13m addressable adult trips a year. Figures: leisure 3.65m – 5.24m; business 1.24m – 1.89m; total 4.89m – 7.13m (p3, TOTAL row); "approximately 4.9 million to 7.1 million trips" (p3, note). Extractor arithmetic, not stated in the source: the twelve row totals sum to 4.895m – 7.13m and the five priority-market rows sum to 4.45m – 6.345m, consistent with the 4.5 million to 6.4 million in the Executive Summary. Focus areas: 6.

10. Western Cape-specific estimates are modelled as a proportional share of South Africa arrivals using Cape Town International Airport's share of foreign air arrivals. Figures: "approximate 34–35% share of foreign air arrivals in South Africa" (p2); "approximately 34–35% share of South Africa's total foreign air arrivals" (p9); "modelled at approximately 34–35% of total South Africa arrivals by air, consistent with Cape Town International Airport's share of foreign air arrivals reported by StatsSA" (p13). Focus areas: 6, 7.

11. South Africa already sits in the Nigerian outbound consideration set, in competition with long-haul aspirational destinations. Figures: "South Africa registers a 10.9% consideration share in Nigerian outbound destination intent data" (p5); "Nigeria competes in an aspirational destination set alongside the USA, Dubai, the UK and France" (p5); profile table "South Africa appears in 10.9% of Nigerian outbound consideration sets" (p5). Focus areas: 6, 1.

12. South Africa ranks second in Kenyan outbound consideration. Figures: "13.4% consideration share in Kenyan outbound destination intent, second only to Dubai" (p6); profile table "South Africa ranks second in Kenyan outbound consideration at 13.4%" (p6). Focus areas: 6.

13. Population bases are stated for three of the four non-South African priority markets; no population figure is given for Kenya (or for South Africa). Figures: Nigeria "approximately 233 million" (p4); Botswana "approximately 2.52 million" (p6 body text and p7 profile table); Zambia "approximately 21.3 million" (p8 body text and profile table). Focus areas: 6.

14. The South African traveller profile is older, affluent, frequent-flying and trade-and-search led. Figures: average age 39.1 years; 44.0% under 35; 57.6% hold a university degree or professional qualification; 75.1% in upper income brackets; 63.6% in the highest income band; average 3.9 international air trips in the past 3 years; 21.5% took 6 or more trips; 63.3% sole decision-maker for international leisure travel; channels: travel agents/tour operators 58.6%, Google Search 67.8%, YouTube 55.7%, Facebook 54.5%, Instagram 49.2% (p4, 4.1 profile table). Focus areas: 6, 1, 3.

15. The Nigerian traveller profile is the most educated of the three surveyed markets and the most Instagram-led. Figures: average age 35.5 years; 54.0% under 35 "— youngest of the three surveyed markets"; 63.6% hold a completed university or postgraduate degree; 49.4% earning KES 600,000+ annually; average 5.5 international air trips in the past 3 years "— highest of the three surveyed markets"; channels: travel agents/tour operators 35.4%, social media advertising 33.8%, Google Search 45.6%, Facebook 42.6%, Instagram 36.5% (p6, 4.3 profile table). Focus areas: 6, 1.

17. Botswana and Zambia profiles are extrapolated, not surveyed, because the Skift database does not cover them. Figures: "the current research scope covers 17 international markets that do not include Botswana" (p7); "Profile extrapolated from South African benchmark; no Botswana-specific Skift survey data available" (p7); "Zambia falls outside the current scope of the Skift 2025 Travel Consumer Market Research database" (p8); "Profile extrapolated from regional context; no Zambia-specific Skift survey data available" (p8). Focus areas: 6, 18.

Focus areas: 6.

19. Tier 4 markets are characterised by population and GDP per capita. Focus areas: 6.

22. Ethiopia's value is framed around Ethiopian Airlines' hub role rather than its own outbound base. Figures: "Ethiopian Airlines is the continent's largest carrier and a global connectivity hub; this creates natural exposure to Cape Town as a transit and onward destination" (p12); "Ethiopia's strategic value lies primarily in Ethiopian Airlines' hub role" (p13); Ethiopia arrivals to SA 7,432 (p12); role "Tier 4 — Hub connectivity market" (p3). Focus areas: 6, 7.

23. Air connectivity is described qualitatively per market, without route or seat data. Figures: Botswana "Short-haul regional market; strong overland and air connectivity to Cape Town" (p7); Zambia "Short-to-medium-haul regional market; one-stop or direct connectivity available" (p8); Kenya positioning "Bleisure itineraries aligned with direct and one-stop connectivity" (p6); Tanzania "strong aviation hub via Dar es Salaam and Kilimanjaro" (p10); Tier 4 markets relevant "as intra-African connectivity expands and middle-class outbound travel behaviour matures across the continent" (p9; the sentence begins on p8); Nigeria strapline labels it "Long-haul premium" (p4), the only market so labelled. Focus areas: 7, 6.

24. South Africa is the market most likely to disperse visitors across the province and across seasons. Figures: "year-round base demand, short-break leisure, school-holiday travel, event-driven visitation, VFR spillover and corporate travel" (p3); "repeat visitors demonstrably more willing to explore the Winelands, Garden Route, Overberg, West Coast and smaller coastal towns beyond Cape Town itself" (p3); positioning "Repeat travel into different seasons and beyond Cape Town into the broader province" (p4); "Year-round demand" (p3, 4.1 strapline). Focus areas: 6, 10, 2, 3.

25. Nigeria and Kenya are positioned for premium leisure and MICE-linked travel. Figures: Nigeria positioning: "Premium and luxury leisure, including five-star hotels, fine dining and exclusive experiences"; "Weddings, milestone travel and celebration trips"; "Bleisure and executive travel"; "Conferences, MICE and trade-linked itineraries"; "Lifestyle retail, nightlife and urban prestige experiences"; "Influencer-led and social-media-first marketing campaigns" (p5). Kenya positioning: "Premium city breaks and short urban escapes"; "Culinary, lifestyle and design-led travel"; "Conference, trade and professional travel with leisure extensions"; "Bleisure itineraries aligned with direct and one-stop connectivity"; "Couples and friend-group travel"; "Digital-first and professionally networked activation" (p6). Focus areas: 6, 2, 1.

26. Travel trade and digital channels are described as complementary across the portfolio. Figures: "All priority markets retain a meaningful reliance on travel agents and tour operators for conversion. Nigeria and South Africa index particularly highly on trade channels"; "digital — particularly Google Search and Instagram across all markets — is increasingly the primary inspiration and research channel" (p13, Section 5 point 6). Focus areas: 6, 3, 1.

27. Cape Town's lifestyle offer is said to match the primary travel motivations across all twelve markets. Figures: "dining and culinary tourism, coastal and scenic experiences, premium hospitality, urban lifestyle, shopping and events" (p13, point 5); Executive Summary "dining, scenery, premium hospitality, events and urban sophistication" (p1). Focus areas: 1, 6.

Figures: "Ghana and Rwanda represent the strongest medium-term leisure growth opportunities within the Tier 4 cohort, given their educated English-speaking traveller bases, growing middle classes and improving air connectivity"; "Wesgro should monitor these markets annually and prioritise Ghana and Rwanda for initial brand-activation investment" (p13, point 4); Rwanda "Smallest population of the seven, but highest-yield potential per capita. Government-led premium tourism strategy (Visit Rwanda), gorilla trekking, MICE-focused Kigali." (p12). Focus areas: 6.

Figures: "None of the Tier 3 or Tier 4 markets are currently covered by the Skift 2025 Travel Consumer Market Research database. Commissioning dedicated consumer survey work for Botswana, Zambia, Ghana and Rwanda in particular would materially improve the precision of targeting" (p13, point 7); "It is considered international best practice to commission destination-specific quantitative and qualitative primary research" (p14). Focus areas: 6, 18.

31. All sizing is directional and is to be validated against official arrivals data. Figures: "No primary research was commissioned for this analysis, and no proprietary demand data was provided by the client" (p1); "These figures should therefore be read alongside — and validated against — official Statistics South Africa and Cape Town Tourism arrivals data where available" (p2); "modelled figures and should be treated as directional rather than definitive" (p13). 33. The memo gives an explicit priority ranking of the five markets, which the tier table does not. Figures: "the combination of lowest booking friction, highest destination familiarity and the strongest repeat visitation potential" (p3); "It is also the market most likely to support geographic dispersal within the province" (p3); Executive Summary "must be treated as the anchor scale market for all leisure, events and business strategies" (p1). 39. The memo frames itself as a baseline, not a forecast. ## Gaps

What the final report's "Build a regional foundation (Africa)" section needs from this document and does not get:

1. Spend per visitor: no spend, receipts, yield or length-of-stay figures for any market, so the "spend per visitor" element of the report brief cannot be sourced here. It will need SAT / Wesgro / StatsSA departure-survey data.
2. Counter-seasonality: no monthly or seasonal arrival profile for any African market. The memo asserts South Africa is "year-round" but gives no data; nothing on whether Nigerian, Kenyan or Ghanaian travel peaks in the Western Cape's green season.
3. Air links: no route, frequency, seat or carrier data (no OAG or ACSA). Direct-route status for Lagos, Nairobi, Accra, Gaborone and Lusaka to Cape Town is described only as "direct and one-stop" or "one-stop or direct".
