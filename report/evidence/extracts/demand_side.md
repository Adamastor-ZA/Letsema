## Findings

1. Cape Town International passenger arrivals reached 1.57m in FY25/26, above the pre-pandemic FY18/19 peak of 1.19m. Source line: CTIA / ACSA, CPT Passenger Statistics. Focus areas: 7; report Chapter 2.1.

2. Western Cape total foreign direct spend rose from R22.7bn in FY2024 to R25.9bn in FY2025 (+14.1%, computed) (p11). Source line: STATS SA, SAT International performance Report, 2025. Focus areas: 5; Chapter 2.1, Chapter 5.

3. The Western Cape takes roughly a quarter of national foreign direct spend from a smaller share of arrivals. WC share of SA arrivals by calendar quarter 2025: Q1 16%, Q2 13%, Q3 13%, Q4 16% (Gauteng 42/41/44/43%; Mpumalanga 24/26/27/25%); WC share of SA spend: Q1 26%, Q2 20%, Q3 22%, Q4 27% (Gauteng 39/44/48/45%; Mpumalanga 7/9/9/8%) (p11). Slide text: "the Western Cape commands roughly a quarter of national foreign direct spend underscoring its premium positioning" (p11). Source: STATS SA, SAT International performance Report, 2025. Focus areas: 5; Chapter 2.1.

4. Demand is seasonally skewed towards late summer. Computed from the printed shares: Oct–Mar = 64.9%; May–Sep = 33.1%; Apr–Sep = 40.0% (all as a share of 104.9, not normalised). Source: STATS SA, SAT International performance Report, 2025. Focus areas: 2; Chapter 2.1, 3.1.

10. The growth model builds rates market by market from four independent levers: 01 Source Market Growth ("The long-run outbound travel growth trend for the origin country. This sets the structural ceiling: WC cannot sustainably grow faster than it's source market is generating travellers"); 02 Western Cape Penetration ("Western Cape penetration as a share of total source market outbound"); 03 Airlift Capacity Ceiling ("Current load factors and probability of new direct routes"); 04 Structural Market Conditions ("Currency competitiveness, visa frictions and market specific macroeconomic context") (p18). Footer: "While source market growth creates the opportunity, air access and market activation ultimately determine how much of that opportunity the Western Cape can capture" (p18). The document calls these "levers" and "factors", not "drivers". Focus areas: 4, 5, 6, 7; Chapter 3.1, 3.4.
