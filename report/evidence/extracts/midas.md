## Definitions and method

- Base year and conventions: calendar years. "2026" figures are scheduled or planned capacity for the year; "2025" bookings are the twelve months to November 2025 (fn1: "Passenger bookings data carries a time lag, usually of around 3-4 months, meaning that the latest available annual data set is for the 12 months to November 2025"). Seasonality uses the IATA winter season. Distances in nautical miles.
- Capacity: scheduled airline seats. The data vendor is not named (no OAG, Cirium or ACSA citation anywhere in the document).
- Bookings and market size: "bookings made through the Global Distribution Systems (GDS's) of airlines around the globe which are then subsequently weighted to provide an estimate of the full market size. These estimates exclude both staff travellers and anyone travelling on an industry rebated ticket which means they will not always reconcile exactly with airport published data that includes every passenger flown" (S3; r5).
- Load factor: estimated bookings divided by capacity ("Estimated Passenger Load Factor %"); the document says load factors "do not necessarily equate to financial performance" (S3; r6).
- Indirect traffic: passengers travelling via another point, used to size unserved markets (S5.2; r16).
- Mainline carrier and LCC are used without definition. "Secondary airport" means not the main airport of its country (S5.1; r13).
- Scope: Western Province/Western Cape airports Capetown, George, Plettenberg Bay (no scheduled services 2026). Benchmarks: Calgary, Christchurch, Edinburgh, Keflavik, Larnaca, Marrakech, Santiago, Valencia.
- Sources the document cites by name: IATA (4% demand growth forecast for 2026); GDS booking data; WESGRO as route-development actor; Vision 2030 (Saudi Arabia). Not cited: Stats SA, ACSA, OAG, SAT, Mero, StratEcon, Census, OSM. Tourist volumes in T1 are unsourced.
- Targets quoted: 2.8 million international visitors by 2035 and 300,000 tourism jobs, "both of which equate to a doubling of current volumes" (S2; r4).
- Internal inconsistencies to carry forward: new routes since 2014 are 14 (S5, F14) and 17 (S1); international routes are 29 (S1, S5) and 27 (F24); Newark load factor 88.5% (S5.2 text) and 88.6% (F24); Middle East share "one in five" (S5) and 23% (F11); Capetown share of SA international capacity "25% today" (S5) against about 23% in 2025 on F12 (read from chart); 35.9 million seats is described both as 2026 scheduled capacity (S3, first use) and as "available capacity in 2025" (S3, load-factor paragraph); Australia paragraph says Qantas operates daily to Johannesburg and also "four times weekly Sydney and three times weekly Perth"; peak season is "November - December" (S5.2) and "November to April" (S1); Capetown capacity about 8.5m on F16 against 13,453,795 on F3; Capetown 2025 capacity "13.3 million" (S4 text) against 13,453,795 (F3); F24 Grand Total 76.0% against 75.0% from summing its 27 rows; Valencia new routes 12 (F19, text) against bars summing to 21 (F20); F25 charts five airlines (Emirates, Ethiopian, SAA, Qatar, Turkish) while the text discusses mostly different ones (Delta, United, BA, KLM, Lufthansa, Qatar, Emirates); benchmark peers are seven in S1 and eight in the body; 20 commercial airports are "planned for 2025" while the surrounding capacity figures are for 2026.
