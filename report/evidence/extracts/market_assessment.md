10. The growth model builds a CAGR per source market from four levers: source market outbound growth ("WC cannot sustainably grow faster than it's source market is generating travellers"), Western Cape penetration of source-market outbound, airlift capacity ceiling ("current load factors and probability of new direct routes") and structural market conditions ("currency competitiveness, visa frictions and market specific macroeconomic context") (p17). FA 4, 7.
11. Three scenarios are defined. Conservative ("The floor"): WC holds share, no new airlift, no above-baseline marketing, India and China as hub-flows, all CAGRs below source-market outbound averages. Base ("The most likely outcome"): current Wesgro trade and marketing at 2025 intensity, selective frequency increases on existing routes, share gains in mature markets, continued hub capacity expansion by Emirates, Ethiopian, Qatar and Turkish, India and China maintain high CAGR, regional Africa continues to grow. Accelerated ("The upside"): new direct routes confirmed to India and China, above-baseline activation in India, China and the USA, hub carriers expand Cape Town capacity, Japan and South Korea introduced, mature European markets grow above base constrained by seat capacity (p18). FA 4, 5, 6, 7.

The subtitle rounds these to "1.82M" and "2.52M" (p19). FA 4, 7.

41. The four stated objectives of the assessment are: (1) "Understand where Western Cape demand comes from today" (baseline by source market, volume, value, seasonality and purpose of travel); (2) "Identify future growth markets and traveller types"; (3) "Prioritise source markets and traveller archetypes" via a structured prioritisation framework; (4) "Inform where Wesgro should focus marketing, airlift and trade enablement" (p6). The framing sentence is "Building a clear demand-led view to guide where Wesgro focuses time, effort and investment" (p6). FA 4, 5, 7, 3.
42. Section 2 key takeouts (p9): headline "WC has a solid tourism performance, but growth remains structurally constrained"; (a) "A small number of mature source markets drive arrivals and spend ... reinforcing stability but also creating concentration risk"; (b) "Not all arrivals deliver the same economic impact ... Growth in arrivals alone risks diluting value"; (c) "Demand remains seasonal adding pressure to peak periods and underutilisation off-season" (p9). FA 5, 6, 2.
43. Value-led framing on the spend and comparator slides: "The highest-spending visitors come predominantly from Europe and North America, meaning future value growth remains tied to markets with modest outbound growth"; "While average spend from Namibia and Zimbabwe is materially lower, these markets contribute stability, frequency and counter-seasonal demand" (p13). "High-arrival destinations like Egypt and Morocco dominate on volume but materially underperform on value per visitor ... arrival growth alone is a weak proxy for economic impact"; "Kenya demonstrates that destinations with lower volumes but premium, long-stay and experience-led tourism can extract significantly more value per visitor" (p14). The comparator set is a 2024 level snapshot only; no comparator growth rates or institutional arrangements are given (p14). FA 5, 6, 2.
44. Section 3 key takeouts (p16): headline "Future growth will not come from a single market or lever, but from a balanced growth portfolio"; (a) "High-growth and high-volume long-haul markets will drive scale and value ... Securing early share in these markets will unlock meaningful volume"; (b) "Regional markets and business travel can counter seasonality: African source markets and a strengthened pipeline of conferences and major events provide structural levers to smooth demand across the calendar"; (c) "Continued optimisation of mature markets will protect and grow the base ... Protecting share, increasing yield and driving regional dispersal will sustain stability while new growth markets are developed" (p16). FA 4, 5, 6, 2, 10.
