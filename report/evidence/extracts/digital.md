## Findings

1. Skift's primary digital research covers four Western Cape destinations, Cape Town, Stellenbosch, Franschhoek and Simon's Town, benchmarked against Marrakesh and Cairo, with the two comparators chosen in consultation with Letsema and the Western Cape destinations chosen for sufficient data volume. Figures: 4 Western Cape destinations, 2 comparators (p1, 1. Introduction); comparators "identified in consultation with Letsema" (p1); adding further competitive destinations "would represent a change in project scope" (p1). Focus areas: 3, 5.

2. The search dataset is Google Trends for 8 December 2024 to 7 December 2025, collected weekly and indexed, for each destination keyword in relation to tourist destination categories (beaches, historical sites, resorts, parks, gardens, zoos), examined for volume trends, geographical breakdown (domestic, regional, international) and associated search terms. Figures: Google Trends 8 December 2024 to 7 December 2025 (p1, 2. Methodology); weekly indexed data (p1); three lenses: Search Volume Trends, Geographical Breakdown, Associated Search Terms (p1 to p2). Focus areas: 3, 18.

3. All search figures are expressed relative to the single highest recorded volume in the dataset; "Cape Town wine" is that benchmark and "Stellenbosch Cape Town" reached 43% of it. Figures: "Cape Town wine" = highest associated search volume among all analysed terms (p2, 2. Methodology); "Stellenbosch Cape Town" = 43% of that benchmark (p2). This is the only numeric result the memo prints. Focus areas: 1, 3.

4. The sentiment dataset is Mentionlytics for 8 December 2024 to 8 December 2025 across eight channels, filtered to travel- and tourism-related mentions; net sentiment is total positive minus total negative mentions over the 12-month period, associated keywords are analysed as bigrams and trigrams, and web mentions were excluded from the keyword stage. Figures: Mentionlytics 8 December 2024 to 8 December 2025 (p2, 2. Methodology); 8 channels: YouTube, Twitter (X), Web, Instagram, Reddit, Bluesky, TikTok, Facebook (p2); 12-month period (p2). Focus areas: 3, 18.

5. Plettenberg Bay was replaced by Simon's Town because Simon's Town data "proved more relevant to current tourism patterns", and Franschhoek was analysed but excluded from the visualisation outputs because of its close similarity to Stellenbosch in sentiment and search behaviour. Figures: 3 destinations visualised (Cape Town, Stellenbosch, Simon's Town) plus 2 comparators (p2, 2. Methodology, Note). Focus areas: 3, 10.

6. Cape Town holds the highest search visibility and peaks in December, which Skift reads as "consistent with its summer holiday seasonality and high inbound interest from regional markets"; Stellenbosch shows secondary peaks in October to November aligned with the wine and events calendar; Cairo peaks in November; Marrakesh generates steadier year-round volumes "due to diversified interest across long-haul markets". Figures: Cape Town peak December (p3, 3. Results Overview, Search Volume and Seasonality); Stellenbosch secondary peaks October to November (p3); Cairo peak November (p3). No indexed values are printed. Focus areas: 2, 3, 5.

7. Domestic and regional searches dominate for Western Cape destinations, with comparatively low European and Middle Eastern search intensity outside Cape Town; Skift labels this "Positive but Shallow Awareness Beyond Africa". Figures: none numeric; statement at p3 (3. Results Overview) and p4 (5. Main Observations, "Positive but Shallow Awareness Beyond Africa"). Cape Town interest is "primarily from within South Africa and neighbouring countries, with limited traffic from Europe and the Middle East" (p3, 4. Destination-Level Insights). Focus areas: 3, 5.

8. Cape Town sentiment is largely positive, but mentions associated with Cape Town International Airport register a more negative tone, which Skift calls "a minor reputational detractor" and links to the need for traveller experience management at key gateways. Figures: none numeric; p3 (3. Results Overview, Digital Sentiment); p4 (5. Main Observations, "Infrastructure Perception Management"). Focus areas: 7, 11, 19.

9. Stellenbosch records slightly lower net sentiment than Cape Town because of a higher share of neutral mentions rather than negativity; negative sentiment "spikes occasionally (e.g., September 2025)" and the spikes are tied to isolated events unrelated to tourism. Figures: example negative spike September 2025 (p3, 3. Results Overview, Digital Sentiment); example cause "news coverage involving South Africans abroad" (p3). The memo gives September 2025 and the news-coverage cause as examples ("e.g."), not as the only spike or the only cause. Focus areas: 3.

10. Simon's Town shows exclusively positive sentiment at relatively low volume, driven by Boulders Beach, penguins and naval heritage, with search traffic and digital visibility that remain highly domestic. Figures: none numeric; p3 (3. Results Overview, Digital Sentiment); p4 (4. Destination-Level Insights, Simon's Town). Skift reads this as "strong destination affinity and potential for high conversion once awareness grows" (p4). Focus areas: 1, 3, 10, 12.

11. Surrounding towns (Stellenbosch, Franschhoek, Paarl, Simon's Town) strengthen Cape Town's brand halo, and travellers' digital associations treat Cape Town and its neighbours as one travel system, which Skift says calls for integrated regional marketing under a unified Western Cape identity. Figures: none numeric; p3 (4. Destination-Level Insights, Cape Town); p4 (5. Main Observations, "Regional Cohesion"); joint Cape Town and Stellenbosch mentions "show synergistic visibility, supporting cross-promotion opportunities" (p3, Stellenbosch). Focus areas: 3, 16, 17.

12. Wine, landscape and wildlife (especially penguins) dominate online conversation across destinations; Cape Town's search associations centre on Table Mountain, wine and #MotherCity; for Stellenbosch "wine and food experiences are dominant interest drivers" and Spier Wine Estate stands out as "a strong brand anchor", with cross-promotion opportunities "within the Cape Winelands and broader Cape Town region". Figures: none numeric; p3 (4. Destination-Level Insights, Cape Town and Stellenbosch); p4 (5. Main Observations, "Wine and Nature as Core Anchors": these themes "provide cohesive storytelling foundations for Wesgro's destination communications"). Focus areas: 1, 3.

13. Marrakesh demonstrates broader geographic reach, with higher search diversification across Europe and Oceania, which Skift presents as "the advantages of a multi-market visibility strategy", while Cairo's interest is steady but regionally concentrated and anchored by heritage sites (Pyramids, Luxor). Figures: none numeric; p4 (4. Destination-Level Insights, Comparative Destinations). Focus areas: 3, 5.

14. Skift recommends that Wesgro institutionalise digital and search analytics tracking, refreshed preferably quarterly, and integrate the results into a dynamic BI dashboard that cross-references marketing campaign outputs and engagement metrics, airline capacity and route data, and hotel occupancy, ADR and booking trends. Figures: refresh "preferably quarterly" (p4, 6. Next Steps); three cross-reference datasets (p5, 6. Next Steps); the method "establishes a replicable analytical framework that can be updated periodically and eventually integrated into a business intelligence dashboard" (p2, 2. Methodology). Focus areas: 3, 7, 18.

15. Skift recommends annual or bi-annual primary quantitative brand perception and traveller behaviour research among representative samples of domestic and international travellers as a complementary data pillar, because digital and search data are directional only and are not a measurement of actual visitation, market performance or economic impact. Figures: "annual or bi-annual primary market research" (p5, 6. Next Steps); four outputs: awareness/perception/intent, segmentation and audience tracking, ROI measurement, comparative brand health benchmarking (p5); "the ideal methodology for deeper insight would involve primary quantitative brand perception and traveller behaviour and attitude research" (p1, 1. Introduction); "directional and indicative" and "should not be interpreted as a comprehensive measurement of actual visitation, market performance, or economic impact" (p5, Disclaimer). Focus areas: 1, 3, 18.

16. Skift's fifth Main Observation, "Experience-Centric Branding Needed", reads Simon's Town's strong sentiment despite limited awareness as evidence that "smaller nodes can be leveraged to diversify the Western Cape offer, appealing to niche travellers seeking authentic, nature-based experiences"; Boulders Beach and penguin experiences "act as powerful emotional and visual hooks for marketing", and Simon's Town's domestic skew is framed as "underscoring opportunities to build international awareness". Figures: none numeric; p4 (5. Main Observations, "Experience-Centric Branding Needed"); p4 (4. Destination-Level Insights, Simon's Town). This is the memo's only statement on dispersal to smaller nodes and is the digital-evidence hook for the community shortlist. Focus areas: 1, 3, 10, 12, 16.

17. Skift frames the memo as "a baseline benchmark for Wesgro to track shifts in traveller interest, digital visibility, and sentiment across key Western Cape destinations relative to peer destinations", to be maintained so Wesgro can judge "how effectively marketing, product development, and air access strategies are resonating with target markets"; the method was designed "to identify measurable indicators of traveller interest, seasonality, and perception that can be tracked over time". Figures: none numeric; p4 (6. Next Steps, opening paragraph); p1 (2. Methodology, opening paragraph); closing paragraph: the digital and primary research streams together "would form a comprehensive destination intelligence framework" (p5, 6. Next Steps). The stated purpose of the research is "to understand traveller perceptions, digital visibility, and interest dynamics across markets relevant to Wesgro's regional tourism strategy" (p1, 1. ## Definitions and method

- Base period: 12 months. Google Trends 8 December 2024 to 7 December 2025 (p1); Mentionlytics 8 December 2024 to 8 December 2025 (p2, p5). Calendar dates; no fiscal-year convention is used. Note: the Disclaimer (p5) applies the 8 December 2024 to 8 December 2025 period to both the Google Search data and Mentionlytics, which differs by one day from the Google Trends end date on p1; cite the Methodology dates for each dataset.
- Search metric: weekly, indexed Google Trends search volume; all figures expressed relative to the highest recorded volume across the dataset (p1 to p2).
- Search keyword scope: each destination keyword in relation to tourist destination categories such as beaches, historical sites, resorts, parks, gardens and zoos (p1).
- Geographical breakdown: relative contribution of domestic, regional and international markets to total search activity (p1).
- Net Sentiment: total positive mentions minus total negative mentions over the 12-month period across all monitored channels (p2).
- Mention filter: only mentions referencing the destination together with travel- and tourism-related keywords (p2).
- Associated keyword analysis: bigrams and trigrams; web mentions excluded at this stage; irrelevant or repetitive terms removed (p2).
- Comparators: Marrakesh and Cairo, "regional and international benchmarks within Africa and the Middle East", chosen in consultation with Letsema (p1, p2).
- Destination substitutions: Plettenberg Bay replaced with Simon's Town; Franschhoek analysed but excluded from visualisation outputs (p2, Note).
- Scope limits: analysis is "directional and indicative"; not a measurement of actual visitation, market performance or economic impact; adding peer destinations is a scope extension (p1, p2, p5). The Disclaimer adds that the data and insights "are subject to change" and that "Skift Inc. accepts no responsibility for business or policy decisions made solely on the basis of this memo without further verification, contextual interpretation, or supporting primary research" (p5).
- Destination coverage wording: the Introduction says "key destinations in the Western Cape, including Cape Town, Stellenbosch, Franschhoek, and Simon's Town" (p1); no other Western Cape destination is named as analysed anywhere in the memo, so four is the analysed set. Paarl is named only as a surrounding town contributing to Cape Town's brand halo (p3), not as an analysed destination.
- Sources the document cites: Google Trends / Google Search (p1, p5); Mentionlytics (p2, p5); "Skift's broader industry knowledge and secondary sources considered reliable at the time of preparation" (p5). None of Stats SA, ACSA, OAG, SAT, Mero, StratEcon, Census or OSM is cited.
- Organisation naming as given: "Skift Inc." (p1 header, p5 Disclaimer); "Skift" elsewhere; "Wesgro"; "Letsema".
