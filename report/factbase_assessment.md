# The three Western Cape factbases: what they add to the report

**For:** Michael
**Subject:** How the three attached factbases can strengthen draft v11 of the Wesgro action plan report
**Date:** 8 November 2026
**Decision (Michael, 8 November 2026):** "No need to reference these docs, just use." Draft v12 therefore uses the factbase content directly, under option (b) below. Facts are cited to the original documents the factbases name, never to the factbases. The primary-document check proposed in sections 2 and 8 is not done. The items in section 7 stay out because they are errors or the compiler's own proposals, not sourcing questions. `build/redteam/v12/decisions.md` sets the treatments.

**Basis:** the three factbases (Demand, Supply, Enablement; Inbound and In-Destination Mobility; Provincial and Municipal), the v11 content and the study's evidence extracts. A reader mapped 157 factbase facts to the report; a verifier checked 66 of them against the factbase text, v11 and the deliverables. Deliverables are abbreviated as in the report's tables: Diag (Current State Diagnostic), DSA (Demand-Side Analysis), IR (Infrastructure Report), SIR (Stakeholder Insights Report) and MA (Market Assessment Report).

## 1. Verdict

The factbases add more than their status as secondary compilations suggests. They carry Wesgro's own FY26 to FY30 targets, the province's and the City's existing tourism, transport and safety plans, district and site data from the Treasury's economic reviews and the study behind the cruise figures. The report says it lacks much of this.

Their extraction can be trusted; their interpretation cannot. In every item checked, the factbase says what the review reports, under a dated page footnote. Whether the primary documents say the same is untested. Two factbases say all their sources were "supplied by the user", and the third labels some counts "assistant calculations". All three read as assistant-built compilations. They present three-year totals as annual, print one table in the wrong units and set the compiler's proposals beside source facts. Our own reviewers' inferences needed correction in about half the items checked.

Most of the primary documents are already on Letsema's file. The Inception Report (p34) lists as received from Wesgro the five-year strategy, the Tourism Blueprint, G4J 2023, PERO 2025, the six MEROs, the City's Tourism Development Framework (TDF), the Air Access "Letsema Update" deck and the 2024/25 cruise study. The factbases are therefore a finding aid. Nothing should enter v12 on their authority alone.

Five changes matter most.

1. **Wesgro's plan reaches the report.** The FY26 to FY30 plan sets six tourism targets (plan p20). The alignment test in 1.1 then rests on numbers, not principle. Two current readings fall: the arrivals metric runs over five years, not ten, and the 250 cruise calls look like a five-year total, not a season.
2. **The plan sharpens the central finding.** Its activity targets roughly hold today's pace, but its arrivals outcome needs 2.6 to 3.3 times the base case's gain to 2030. That supports the claim that growth is a supply programme and gives Chapter 4 a concrete alignment task.
3. **District and site data fill Pillar 2 and Chapter 5 gaps.** MERO counts all five flagship sites. Cape Point is among the busiest, not "underused". Tourist spend equals 15.6% to 16.8% of GDPR in four outer districts against 6.7% in the Metro.
4. **Existing programmes change Chapter 4's owners and partners.** The City has designed a Tourist Safety Programme. Wesgro has an Advocacy and Advisory unit. DEDAT holds a ranked visa reform study. The Department of Mobility's draft framework plans to revive the airport bus. The plan should build on these, not ask partners to start them.
5. **Cruise gets its source.** StratEcon shows R1.79bn is GDP-R, not "impact". International passengers rose 81% over three seasons while the total fell 12% (two sources, to verify). Jobs were 1,234 direct and 2,345 in total. In 2023/24, 20 of 67 calls were Red Sea reroutes.

I recommend option (b) in section 8: close open items and fix conflicts, then enrich Chapters 2 to 5, after a two-day read of the primary documents on file. It adds six to nine pages.

### Overlaps and contradictions

The factbases share six documents, and the overlaps work as cross-checks. The cruise jobs question resolves. The municipal factbase gives "2,345 provincial jobs"; the mobility factbase, reading the same study, gives 1,234 direct and 2,345 total (StratEcon pp24, 27). The Diagnostic's 1,234 (p37) is most likely the direct figure, not a slip. The cruise base resolves too: the plan's 67 calls for 2023/24 match StratEcon and the Diagnostic, and its 47-call average equals 67 less the 20 reroutes.

Two problems surface. The DSE factbase places PERO's CapeNature count and 3.4 million domestic trips at pp87 and 91; the municipal factbase places the same facts at pp69 to 70. Every PERO page needs checking. The PLTF profile also puts Germany at 23.8% and the USA at 6.2% of international visitors. PERO gives the UK 16.4%, the USA 15.0% and Germany 14.5% of 2024 CTIA arrivals, broadly in line with the report's own sources. The PLTF profile is the outlier.

### The rule for using them

1. A factbase figure enters v12 cited to its primary source, with page; never to the factbase.
2. Where the primary is on file, someone reads the page first.
3. Where it is not (TSMA, BDO), the figure carries an open-item flag in the report's style, such as "[to confirm against TSMA, Nov 2025, p3]", and the document is requested.
4. Values this review computed carry "(computed)".
5. Compiler proposals (indicator lists, typologies, "implications") enter only as Letsema recommendations.
6. Nothing is cited to a factbase alone unless you accept that for a named item.

## 6. New findings, and recommendations they challenge

The factbases support four findings the report does not yet make.

First, Wesgro's plan roughly holds today's pace of activity but asks for a step change in outcome. Its targets imply about 32 bids a year against 36 in 2024/25; 50 calls a year against 79 vessels (on a base that excludes reroutes); 2 new routes a year against 1.7 since 2015; and 2,500 trade engagements a year, flat on FY24. Its arrivals metric is 2.6 to 3.3 times what the base case adds by 2030 and about twice what the accelerated case adds (computed). On that reading no modelled path delivers it. This is the Board-level case that growth needs supply, not more promotion. It waits on p20.

Second, the conversion gap can be measured. The province captured about 0.1% of global outbound trips over 2022 to 2024 and no more than 0.27% of air outbound trips in any core market (TSMA pp2, 4). Most Indian and Chinese visitors to South Africa never reach the Cape.

Third, cruise value comes from mix, not volume. International passengers rose 81% while total passengers fell 12%, and GDP-R rose from R1.234bn to R1.789bn (StratEcon; Diag p37).

Fourth, concentration shows in visitors as well as assets. Nine of the ten places international tourists visit most are in the Metro. The 2023 rebound went to the Metro while the Garden Route fell 20%. Domestic trips fell 6.1% from 2020 to 2024 as prices rose (PERO). International growth can price out the domestic visitors who fill the green season and the regions. The plan should say so.

Two recommendations follow from Wesgro's and the province's own framing. Wesgro's tourism outcome is "growth in leisure tourism consideration" (plan p19), yet consideration is not the constraint; the outcome should move toward conversion and value. G4J treats tourism as a service export inside a goal to triple exports (pp11, 14), so alignment can rest on value as well as arrivals. Both are our interpretation and wait on the primary text.
