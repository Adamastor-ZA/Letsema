# Green season: evaluation and proposed amendments to v14.1

Source: "Western Cape tourism additional research and delivery priorities", updated 10 November 2026, new section 3.1 "Green season: build on progress already achieved" (pages 11 to 16). Nothing else in the pack changed. Chapter 15 and chapters 1 to 14 read as before.

Nothing in v14.1 has been changed. This note proposes the changes for your decision.

## 1. Verdict

The research changes the story more than the plan. v14.1 presents the green season as a trough to fill. The time series shows a market that has already doubled, and whose share of the year rose and then eased. Objective 2 and I2 stay as they are. What changes is the framing, the evidence under it and the way progress is measured.

Three findings do the work.

1. **The quieter months are a proven market.** April to September passenger movements at Cape Town International rose from 662,771 in 2015 to 1,342,770 in 2025, up 102.6%. The other six months grew 85.7%.
2. **The balance improved, then slipped.** The April to September share of the year rose from 38.7% in 2015 to 41.6% in 2023, and eased to 40.8% in 2025. Since 2019 the green months have grown 24.4% and the other six months 27.8%.
3. **Rooms have not followed the traffic.** Cape Town sample occupancy averaged 51.8% from May to August 2025. That is above 2024 (50.5%) and below 2023 (58.1%) and 2019 (54.5%). June 2025 was 45.1%.

The third finding is the one v14.1 does not have. It also closes an open item: the report has been waiting for June 2025 occupancy.

## 3. How far the findings reach

The airport series is sound and the accommodation evidence is usable with care. The series comes from one release, uses one definition throughout and marks the pandemic years. Checks on arrivals only and on international traffic only give the same reading.

Four limits matter for the report.

1. **Movements are an access measure.** They show that more people pass through the terminal in the quieter months. They do not show more visitor nights or more spend.
2. **Traffic and rooms point in different directions.** Green-month movements in 2025 were 24.4% above 2019, while May to August sample occupancy was 2.7 points below it. More room stock, shorter stays, resident outbound travel and sample change could each explain part of that. The research does not test which. Section 2.3 of v14.1 already notes that short-term rentals are a large and uncounted layer of supply, which is one candidate.
3. **A share can mislead.** The share eased in 2024 and 2025 while volume grew, because the other six months grew faster. A share can also rise because summer weakens. On my arithmetic from the research table, the sample's October to March occupancy averaged 69.5% in 2025, against 77.0% in 2023 and 71.7% in 2019. The peak months softened too.
4. **Nothing is attributed.** The series cannot credit Cape Town Air Access, a campaign or an event for the growth. Nor does it size the unmet demand.

The result is therefore a correction of emphasis. Further green-season growth is credible because it has happened before. It is not yet a quantified opportunity.

## 6. What I would not change

- **Objective 2, its name and I2.** The research extends I2; it does not ask for a parallel programme.
- **The seat evidence.** June's 6.4% of seat capacity and the monthly split of overseas visitors stand. The series adds a trend beside them.
- **The first winter portfolio.** May to August inside April to September is the research's own sensitivity case.
- **Domestic and visiting-friends offers.** They stay in 1.3 as context (P4).
- **The research's dated programme, its owners and its room-night arithmetic** (P3). The 615 room-night illustration is arithmetic, not evidence.
- **The organiser's R41 million estimate for the Knysna festival.** It is not validated, so the proposals do not use it.

## 7. Decisions for Michael

1. **Apply as v14.2?** I recommend it. The changes are about 30 edits with no new initiative, action or indicator.
2. **Length.** v14.1 is 209 pages against a target of 200 to 210. I estimate these changes add about one page, which reaches the top of the range. If you want headroom, the full table can go to Appendix A2 with only the headline figures in 2.2.
3. **Rename T1-05?** "Green-season nights and share" changes a Tier 1 name in six tables. The alternative keeps the name and changes only the target wording.
4. **Table 3.2 length.** It would run to twelve rows, with three added and two merged. Say if you would rather drop the analyst estimate for the regions.
5. **The December and January sentence in 2.3.** It says the peak "overwhelms transport and accommodation capacity". The Cape Town sample shows hotels at 66.6% in January and 68.4% in December 2025. The sentence rests on a stakeholder statement and may describe a region, not Cape Town. I have not proposed a change. Options: leave it, attribute it to its source, or narrow it to the place it describes.
6. **The traffic and rooms gap.** I propose stating it and naming the possible causes without choosing one. If you want it tested, the accommodation audit in I10 and the room-night baseline in I2 are the routes.

## 8. One caution on balance

The amendments should not swing the report to "seasonality is solved". June to August remain well below the peak months, the share has slipped for two years and winter rooms are not back to 2019. The proposed wording holds both halves: a market already doubled, and a balance still to win.
