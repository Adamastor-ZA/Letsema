# Item 15: evaluation and proposed amendments to v14

Source: "Western Cape tourism additional research and delivery priorities", updated 10 November 2026, chapter 15 "Regional product visibility, distribution and bookability" (pages 39 to 48). Chapters 1 to 14 are unchanged from the 9 November version, which v14 already reflects.

Nothing in v14 has been changed. This note proposes the changes for your decision.

## 1. Verdict

The research corrects the diagnosis, not the objective. Regional accommodation is present on the booking platforms, so "hard to book" overstates what the evidence shows. What remains unmeasured is whether that product is found, has rooms on the dates travellers want and converts into sales. Objective 3 and initiative I3 stay. Their logic changes from "count the gap, then onboard" to "audit the path from discovery to sale, then support the failure the audit finds".

v14 is partly there already. v13 removed the claim that rural properties are absent from online travel agencies, and v14 says accommodation is widely listed. Yet v14 still says "hard to book" in six prominent places, plans an onboarding drive before any audit and measures listing coverage, which the test suggests is the wrong thing to count.

## 2. What the research tested and found

The test was a desk audit of 36 named cases, fixed before any channel was checked, on 10 November 2026.

| Test | Result | What it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Accommodation: 24 cases, 21 eligible | 13 of 13 outside the core and 8 of 8 core comparators had a property-specific platform listing. Outside the core, 11 had a Booking.com trace and 11 an opened domestic listing. Three cases were held, not replaced | Regional rooms are listed. A blanket absence claim fails |
| Attractions: 12 cases | 3 of 8 outside the core appeared on an experience marketplace, each as a stop on a tour. None showed a standalone online ticket. All 4 core comparators had an operator-directed online purchase route | Experiences sell differently outside the core. The gap v14 describes for experiences survives as a hypothesis |
| Prominence, availability and sales | Not tested. No ranking, travel-date, checkout or conversion check was run | Whether regional product is disadvantaged in real discovery and purchase stays open |

Two further findings matter for the plan.

- **Ranking has a price.** Booking.com discloses that commission and programme participation can influence ranking, and the Competition Commission examined sponsored ranking in its 2023 platforms inquiry. A listing can therefore exist and still earn little exposure, or earn it at a cost to the operator.
- **A tour stop is not a regional night.** The three regional attractions found on a marketplace are stops on Cape Town day tours. That is exposure. It does not show a local receipt or a night spent in the region.

## 3. How far the finding reaches

The test is sound for what it claims and narrow in what it covers. Its method is careful: the cohort was fixed first, held cases were not swapped out and an unverified match was never coded as an absence. A sensitivity check drops the three boundary towns (McGregor, Tulbagh and Calitzdorp) and all ten remaining cases still show a listing.

Four limits decide how v14 should use it.

1. **The cohort favours businesses that are easy to find.** It was chosen, not sampled, and leans towards established hotels, collections and guest farms. It does not represent informal, community or unlisted supply. It cannot give a provincial percentage.
2. **Every Booking.com positive is an index trace.** No property page on that platform was opened. The opened evidence is on a domestic platform.
3. **Exact-name search is not a traveller's search.** The test asked whether a known property leaves a trace. It did not ask whether an undecided visitor would meet it.
4. **The growth-market platforms were not audited.** Trip.com, Fliggy, MakeMyTrip and Almosafer are untested, and those are the channels objective 3 leans on for China, India and the Gulf.

The result is therefore one-sided. It disproves "regional product is absent". It does not disprove "many small operators and community sellers lack an online route", and it says nothing yet about prominence or sales.

## 6. What I would not change

- **Objective 3, its name and I3's number.** The research itself retains the objective of a findable and bookable province.
- **The community evidence.** The test does not cover community or informal supply, so the Inclusion Plan's findings and the I12 booking routes stand.
- **The sequence "seats and bookability move first".** The audit is a first-half 2027 action, as the census was.
- **The route ratings in Table 2.7.** They are the Infrastructure Report's judgement-based ratings and are already labelled as such in Appendix A4.
- **The pack's dates, owners and funding notes for the audit** (P3). The proposals use the report's own phases.

## 7. Decisions for Michael

1. **Apply now as v14.1, or hold for v15?** I recommend v14.1. The changes are contained: about 30 edits, no new initiative, action or indicator, and roughly one extra page (207 to about 208).
2. **Name the 36 cases?** I recommend counts only in the report. The case register names individual businesses and sits better in the research record. An appendix table of counts by cohort is an option if you want the test visible.
3. **The 40% estimate.** I recommend keeping it, labelled as unscoped, so the audit has a stated claim to test. The alternative is to drop it from 2.3 and Table 3.3.
4. **T2-04's new definition.** It becomes a two-part measure (usable booking route; exposure). Say if you would rather keep one number and report exposure only in the audit.
5. **Does the executive summary lead change?** A1 softens the report's opening line. It is the most visible edit and carries a Word comment thread, which would be preserved.

## 8. One caution on balance

The amendments should not swing the report to "regional product is well distributed". The test cannot support that either. The proposed wording holds both halves in each place: rooms are listed, and reach and sales are unmeasured.
