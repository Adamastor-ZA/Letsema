# Prompt: Wesgro Doubling Tourism Research, Summary of Findings Deck

Paste everything below the line into a fresh session, with the input files attached.

---

## 1. Your role and the job

You are a senior engagement manager at Letsema Consulting and Advisory, working on the Wesgro "Doubling Tourism Research Initiative" (RFP SCM002-2025). Build a PowerPoint (.pptx) interim "Summary of Findings" pack, on the Wesgro template and corporate identity, for a team working session.

The session is a reflection and alignment exercise, not a results presentation. Many deliverables were completed some time ago. The pack must bring the findings of every workstream into one place, test them against the original hypothesis tree, expose what has not yet been analysed (marketing in particular), and give the group a structured basis to challenge the conclusions and agree a set of responses. After the session we move into drafting the final conclusions and recommendations, so the pack must make that next step easy.

The core question the study asks is how Wesgro and its partners double international tourist arrivals to the Western Cape by 2035 (1.4m to 2.8m), and what must be true, and who must act, for that to happen. Every slide earns its place by moving the reader closer to an answer.

## 2. Context you must hold

**The client.** Wesgro is the Western Cape's tourism, trade and investment promotion agency and the destination marketing organisation. Its FY26 to FY30 plan targets an additional 800,000 to 1 million international arrivals over five years, as a milestone toward doubling by 2035. The stated upside is 150,000 to 300,000 tourism-linked jobs, 10,000 SMMEs connected to global markets, and a doubling of airlift and cruise volumes. The work supports the Western Cape Growth for Jobs strategy.

**The thesis in the RFP.** Doubling requires more than marketing. It needs an ecosystem-wide response: targeted market selection and destination marketing; enablement (visas, flight capacity); infrastructure investment including PPPs; technology; skills and SMMEs; a broader suite of experiences; mega events; inclusivity; and destination reputation. Priority new markets are China, ASEAN, India, Brazil and the Middle East, alongside established markets (EU, UK, US, Africa). The RFP names five barriers: incomplete understanding of source markets, infrastructure constraints in priority nodes, weak community and SMME participation, poor tourism intelligence systems, and limited adoption of sustainable and carbon-smart models.

**The contract (SLA signed 9 and 11 December 2025).** Letsema, represented by Michael Harris (Senior Partner). Wesgro, represented by Wrenelle Stander (CEO). Value R3.68m including VAT. Term 1 November 2025 to 31 October 2026. Payment is milestone-based against these deliverables: Inception and Project Kick-off; Current State Diagnostic Report; Route-to-Market Research and Demand Analysis (Market Strategy Report); Spatial and Infrastructure Assessment (Infrastructure Investment Framework); SMME and Inclusion Strategy (Inclusive Tourism and SMME Blueprint); Monitoring and Evaluation Framework (M&E Toolkit); Global Benchmarking (Benchmarking Report); Stakeholder Engagement and Interim Presentations (10 interactions); Final Research Report and Knowledge Transfer; and Launch Event and Knowledge Transfer Activities (4).

**The audience.** The full project team and any Wesgro participants the user confirms. They are intelligent, time-poor and close to the detail of their own workstream. They have not recently seen the whole picture. Write for people who will challenge you.

## 3. Inputs

Attached or to be attached: the RFP (SCM002-2025), the signed SLA, the Wesgro PowerPoint template, the original hypothesis tree, each completed workstream deliverable, and a small number of additional assets (for example stakeholder interview notes, data extracts or earlier steering committee material).

**Step one, before building anything.** Read every input in full. Then produce a short input register in your reply (not in the deck) listing each file, what it is, its date, its status (final, draft, partial), and which hypothesis-tree branch it informs. Flag anything missing, contradictory between documents, or out of date. If the hypothesis tree or the Wesgro template is missing, stop and ask for it. Do not invent a tree or approximate the brand.

**Source discipline.** Every number, quote and conclusion in the deck must trace to a named input. Cite the source on the slide in small text (document name, section or page). If a figure cannot be traced, do not use it. Where workstreams disagree (for example differing market sizes or baselines), show the disagreement on a slide rather than silently picking one.

## 4. Output specification

**Format.** One .pptx file, 16:9, built on the supplied Wesgro template. Use its slide masters, layouts, fonts, colour palette, logo placement and footer conventions exactly. Do not recolour, restyle or add decorative elements outside the template. Use native, editable PowerPoint objects (text boxes, tables, shapes, charts) so the team can edit live. No flattened images of text or tables. Where you build the hypothesis tree as a diagram, build it from native shapes and connectors.

**Length.** 22 to 28 content slides plus an appendix. This is a discussion pack; density should support conversation, not a read-ahead report. Add a small number of discussion prompts rather than long text.

**Writing style (applies to every word on every slide and in the speaker notes).**
- UK English throughout (organisation, prioritise, programme, analyse).
- No em dashes and no en dashes in prose. Use a full stop, semicolon, comma or brackets instead.
- No Oxford comma.
- Each content slide has a short ALL-CAPS topic header, then one topline sentence directly beneath it that states what the slide argues (a claim, not a label). Supporting points are full clauses that evidence or develop the topline; they do not restate it.
- Strong verbs, concrete nouns, named places and figures. No intensifiers (very, really, truly). Avoid: leverage, synergy, unlock, ecosystem (unless literal), navigate, landscape, journey, game-changer, robust, holistic.
- Short sentences. Toplines under 25 words where possible.
- Source line in small text at the foot of each evidence slide.
- Mark the deck "DRAFT FOR DISCUSSION" using the template's draft convention or a subtle watermark.
- Speaker notes on every content slide: the intended talking point (2 to 4 sentences), the question to put to the room, and the source detail.

## 5. Storyline and slide architecture

Anchor the whole deck on the hypothesis tree. Build in this order; adjust numbers to fit but keep the logic.

**A. Frame (3 to 4 slides)**
1. Title slide.
2. Purpose of the session and what we need from the room: the three outcomes (challenge the conclusions, close the gaps, agree the shape of the final recommendations). Include a short agenda with timings.
3. The core question and the target: what Wesgro wants to achieve by 2035, the 5-year milestone, and the stakes (arrivals, jobs, SMMEs, airlift and cruise). One slide, one idea.
4. The original hypothesis tree in full, as a native diagram. State plainly that it is the spine of the rest of the deck. Use the tree exactly as the team originally wrote it; if wording has drifted in later documents, note that in the speaker notes.

**B. What we found, by branch (the bulk of the deck, roughly 12 to 14 slides)**

For each branch of the hypothesis tree (and in the order of the tree), provide a consistent pair of slides:
- **Findings slide.** Topline states the finding. Evidence beneath it: the two to four strongest data points or insights from the relevant deliverable, with sources. Include one chart or table where the data supports it (use native charts).
- **Verdict slide or panel.** For each hypothesis in that branch: the hypothesis as originally stated; the verdict (Confirmed, Partly confirmed, Not supported, Not yet tested); evidence strength (Strong, Moderate, Thin); and the implication in one sentence. Use a consistent visual rating (for example a small coloured marker drawn from the Wesgro palette plus a text label, so it survives greyscale printing).

Map the branches to the contracted workstreams as follows, subject to what the tree actually contains: market demand and readiness (Market Strategy Report, including India and China route-to-market); access and connectivity (visas, airlift, cruise); spatial and infrastructure readiness (Infrastructure Investment Framework, ROI); SMME and community inclusion (Blueprint); global benchmarking (lessons, role players, risks); measurement and intelligence (M&E Toolkit); and stakeholder input (interviews, focus groups and roundtables). Add the Current State Diagnostic as the baseline where it is the better home for a finding.

**C. Step back and synthesise (3 to 4 slides)**
- **Hypothesis scorecard.** One slide showing the whole tree with every hypothesis colour-coded by verdict and evidence strength. This is the single most important slide in the deck. A reader should see in ten seconds where we are confident, where we are exposed and where we have not looked.
- **Cross-cutting themes.** The three to five patterns that run across workstreams (for example a constraint that appears in market, infrastructure and SMME findings alike). State each as a claim with evidence from at least two workstreams.
- **Tensions and trade-offs.** Where findings pull against each other (for example prioritising new markets versus protecting established ones; investment sequencing versus the pace of the target). Frame as questions for the room, not resolutions.
- **The "so what".** A first, explicit attempt at the answer to the core question: what must be true by 2030 and 2035 for the target to be credible, and which levers matter most. Label it a working position, not a conclusion.

**D. Gaps (2 to 3 slides)**
- **Gap map.** Compare the tree and the RFP scope with the evidence we hold, and list every area with thin or no analytical coverage. Test at least these against the RFP: destination marketing and brand (named as a known gap), destination reputation and safety perception, mega events and business events, product and experience diversification, technology and digital, skills and workforce, sustainability and carbon-smart models, tourism intelligence systems, cruise, and the roles of national, provincial and local government. Add any gaps you identify yourself from the documents. Do not pad; only list a gap if the inputs genuinely fail to cover it.
- **Marketing deep-dive framing.** Because marketing has had little analytical focus, give it its own slide: what the Market Strategy Report already implies about positioning, channels, partners and spend; what is missing; the specific questions we need answered; and two or three options for closing the gap (for example desk research against benchmark destinations, interviews with trade and distribution partners, or a focused workshop), with a view on effort and what each would deliver before the final report.
- **Gap-closing options.** For each material gap, propose a response (analyse, interview, benchmark, reframe as a limitation, or deliberately scope out), an owner role and the effort required, for the room to agree or amend.

**E. Toward the final recommendations (3 to 4 slides)**
- **Proposed structure of the final report and recommendations.** Suggest how the conclusions should be organised (for example by lever, by market, by actor, or by time horizon), with the argument for the option you favour and one alternative. Show how the roadmap requirement in the SLA (stakeholder roles across national, provincial, district and local government and the private sector, with timelines and risks) will be met.
- **Emerging recommendation themes.** Five to seven draft themes, each in one sentence, tagged with the evidence strength behind it and the hypotheses it rests on. Mark clearly which are firm and which depend on closing a gap.
- **Discussion guide.** Six to eight sharp questions for the room, grouped under: Are we answering the right question? Do the findings hold up? What are we missing? What would we be willing to recommend, and to whom? Keep each to one line.
- **Decisions requested today.** A short list of decisions or alignment points we want to leave the room with.

**F. Next six weeks (1 to 2 slides)**
A Gantt-style roadmap on a single slide, built from native shapes or a table, showing weekly columns for six weeks from the week of the session. Rows: closing the agreed gaps (including marketing); drafting final conclusions and recommendations; M&E Toolkit finalisation (if outstanding); stakeholder engagement and steering committee touchpoints; Final Research Report drafting, review and sign-off (project steering committee, Exco, Board); knowledge transfer and the launch event and roundtable; and project management and SLA milestone sign-offs. Show milestones as diamonds, dependencies as arrows, and decision points (including this session's outcomes) clearly. Beneath it, a short list of the three biggest risks to the timeline and how we will manage them.

**Important timing point to handle explicitly.** The SLA term ends on 31 October 2026, and a six-week plan from mid-October runs into late November. Do not paper over this. Reflect the actual dates of the session (ask the user for the date if it is not given; today is 9 October 2026), show the contract end date as a vertical marker on the roadmap, and add a visible note that work beyond 31 October needs an agreed extension under SLA clause 4.3 (Wesgro's written notice to extend, plus a written addendum, subject to supply chain management provisions). Flag in your reply that the user should confirm the extension position with Wesgro before the pack is circulated, and do not state in the deck that an extension has been agreed unless an input says so.

**G. Appendix**
Hypothesis-by-hypothesis evidence table (hypothesis, verdict, evidence, source, confidence); the input register; a glossary of terms and acronyms; the deliverable status tracker against the SLA milestones (delivered, in review, outstanding); and any detail that supports a main-body slide but would clutter it.

## 6. Analytical standards

- **Verdicts must be earned.** Rate a hypothesis "Confirmed" only where at least two independent sources or methods support it. Use "Not yet tested" honestly; it is more useful than a weak "Partly confirmed".
- **Separate finding from inference.** A finding is what the data shows. An implication is what we think it means. Keep them visibly distinct so the room can challenge the second without disputing the first.
- **Quantify where possible.** Prefer a number, a range or a named comparator to an adjective. Show units, base years and sources. Where figures conflict across deliverables, reconcile or flag.
- **Test the tree itself.** If the evidence suggests a branch is missing, mis-specified or double-counted, say so on the scorecard slide and in the gap map. The tree is a tool, not a sacred text.
- **Keep the target in view.** Where the evidence suggests the 2035 target, or its 2030 milestone, is stretched or poorly specified, say so plainly and show the evidence. Do not soften a finding because it is awkward for the client.
- **Be even-handed on risk.** Include global disruptive factors and delivery risks identified in the benchmarking work.
- **Avoid overreach.** Do not draw conclusions the inputs cannot support. Where a gap prevents a conclusion, say what would be needed to close it.

## 7. Visual standards

- Use the Wesgro template's layouts for every slide; do not create off-template layouts.
- One idea per slide. No slide carries more than roughly 90 words of body text excluding tables and source lines.
- Prefer native charts, simple tables and the hypothesis tree diagram to icons or clip art. No stock imagery unless the template includes it.
- Use colour with intent: the Wesgro palette for structure, and one consistent rating scheme for verdicts. Check contrast and readability at projection size (minimum 12pt body text, 10pt for sources).
- Keep alignment, spacing and font use consistent across the deck. Titles do not wrap to three lines.

## 8. Quality control before you finish

1. Render every slide to an image and inspect it. Fix overflow, overlap, clipped text, broken connectors and misaligned objects.
2. Check every slide against the style rules: UK spelling, no em or en dashes, no Oxford commas, topline present, source present.
3. Check that every hypothesis in the tree appears on the scorecard and in the appendix table, with a verdict and an evidence rating.
4. Check that every number on a slide matches its source.
5. Check that the roadmap dates are consistent with the session date and the 31 October 2026 contract end.
6. Confirm the file opens cleanly, uses the template masters, and that speaker notes are present.

## 9. What to return

1. The .pptx file, saved in the working directory.
2. A short cover note (under 300 words, in plain prose) covering: the input register in one compact table; anything missing or contradictory; the three findings you consider most important and the three areas of greatest weakness; the assumptions you made; and the questions you need the user to answer before the pack is circulated (including the session date and the contract extension position).
3. Do not summarise the deck slide by slide in your reply.

If any instruction above conflicts with what the inputs show, follow the inputs, state the conflict in the cover note and carry on. If something critical is missing, ask before building.
