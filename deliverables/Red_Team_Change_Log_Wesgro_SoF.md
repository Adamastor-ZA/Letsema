## Cover note

The red team found five critical and nineteen major issues; all are fixed in v2, and the deck grew by one slide. The most consequential change is to the green-season argument. The June figure of 6.4% is a share of scheduled seats, which means airlines have withdrawn winter capacity rather than left it idle. The twelve facts, the seasonality slide, the themes and the first priority move now say that green-season demand is what keeps seats in the schedule and fills the venues and beds that are idle. The benchmark slide is corrected (Melbourne is a forecast inside the global norm; Kenya's yield now uses one base), the gap map cites real SLA clauses, the status topline matches its table, and every India and China volume is labelled as a two-way GDS estimate. A new counter-case slide states the strongest evidence against "capture-poor, not demand-poor", attributes the phrase to the client factbase, and gives a falsification test.

Three findings could not be fully fixed from the inputs: no priority move can be costed (no deliverable sizes anything, so each carries a cost line pointing to the gap plan); the route-fund recommendation still rests on benchmark precedent rather than a Western Cape business case; and figures reached through the AI-assisted factbases (capture shares, the 1.6% public-transport figure, cruise totals, the eVisa funnel) are labelled but not yet verified against their primary reports.

Claims that need confirmation before circulation: Wesgro Air Access should confirm whether airlines agree seats or bilaterals bind first, and the fund's feasibility; the Demand-Side authors should confirm the Kenya yield base; the Benchmark authors should state what the NZ$55m "ROI" measures; the Infrastructure Report authors should confirm the swapped category labels on pp27-29 and remove the placeholder text on p15; and Wesgro should supply the TSMA, PLTF and cruise report so the factbase figures can be checked at source.
