## 2. Red team verdict

The deck is not yet fit to present, but the distance is short: one working day of corrections. The structure, the hypothesis-tree spine, the tree markers, the inclusion evidence and most of the numbers survive a hostile reading.

Three problems are serious. First, the green-season argument inverts its own evidence: a 6.4% June seat share means airlines have withdrawn capacity, not that seats sit idle, so the first of the twelve facts and the first priority move need a different anchor. Second, the benchmark slide is unsafe as written: the "two to seven times" topline fails for Chile and Melbourne, Melbourne's growth is a forecast, and the Kenya yield mixes two bases while two deliverables disagree. Third, the gap map cites SLA clauses that do not exist, and the status slide's topline contradicts its table; both are the kind of error a client lead will find within minutes.

The central thesis survives in weakened form. "Capture-poor, not demand-poor" holds for distribution, bookability, communities and hub traffic. It does not hold for airline economics, price, safety perception or funding, all of which the inputs document and the deck leaves out. The thesis also borrows its phrase from an AI-assisted factbase without saying so. It needs a counter-case and softer wording, not abandonment.
